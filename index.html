CLIN-SUMM: Incremental Longitudinal Summarization of Clinical Notes Enables Scalable Representation and Early Disease Prediction

D'Souza, V.; Pace, D. F.; Azhir, A.; Nargesi, A.; Holbrook, E. B.; He, W.; Naumann, T.; Friedman, S.; Atlas, S. J.; Anderson, C. D.; Hung, J.; Maddah, M.

Electronic health records contain years of longitudinal clinical notes rich in evolving patient information, yet their volume, redundancy, and fragmentation limit clinical usability and scalable modeling. We present CLIN-SUMM (Clinical Longitudinal Insight from Notes using Summarization), a framework that restructures summarization as a longitudinal representation problem. Rather than collapsing histories into static summaries, CLIN-SUMM incrementally constructs structured, categorized, date-partitioned patient representations, summarizing only newly documented information at each encounter while preserving temporal fidelity without access to future data. This standardized representation can be computed once and reused across downstream tasks, thereby decoupling narrative processing from prediction. Across 12,356 Massachusetts General Hospital patients, CLIN-SUMM achieved 75% word reduction while maintaining high clinician-rated correctness and completeness. Using dementia as a case study, fine-tuning Clinical ModernBERT on CLIN-SUMM summaries yielded AUROC 0.84 for diagnosis and outperformed raw notes for 3-year risk prediction (AUROC 0.66 vs. 0.63). Combining summary-based predictions with structured EHR variables improved 3-year prediction to AUROC 0.69, reaching 0.74 among patients younger than 75 years, with longitudinal analyses demonstrating progressive risk separation years before a formal diagnosis. CLIN-SUMM summaries also enabled efficient extraction of longitudinal medication trajectories, complementing structured EHR data by recovering medication information documented in notes, with high dose agreement for matched records. CLIN-SUMM provides a scalable representation layer for clinical review and longitudinal machine learning, enhancing disease modeling, risk prediction, and other longitudinal reasoning tasks.

AI-Based MRI Radiomics for Preoperative Detection of Tumor Deposits in Rectal Cancer: A Bivariate Diagnostic Accuracy Meta-analysis

Alsaied, M. A.; Hegazy, M.; Saleh, M. M.; Barakat, B.; Ibrahim, O. R.; Abdelrazek, H. M.; Nazemi, S.; El-Hussuna, A.; Shalaby, M.

Background: In rectal cancer (RC), tumor deposits (TDs) are clinically important as they are associated with a negative impact on prognosis and affect risk stratification as well as management plans. MRI radiomics combined with artificial intelligence (AI) has been explored for non-invasive preoperative TDs detection. However, results vary across studies; therefore, we performed a systematic review and meta-analysis to pool their diagnostic test accuracy (DTA). Methods: This systematic review and DTA meta-analysis were conducted in accordance with the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Diagnostic Test Accuracy and reported in adherence to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses of Diagnostic Test Accuracy Studies (PRISMA-DTA). Databases were screened for DTA studies of MRI-based radiomics predicting TDs in adult RC patients with histopathology as reference. Results: Nine studies (2589 patients) were identified. Across included studies, the bivariate meta-analysis showed pooled sensitivity of 0.765 (95% CI 0.714 - 0.809) and specificity of 0.790 (95% CI 0.750 - 0.825), with overall AUC of 0.845 and very low heterogeneity (I2 = 7.3%). In validation datasets, diagnostic performance remained consistent (AUC 0.806), with pooled sensitivity 0.751 (95% CI 0.681 - 0.809) and specificity 0.751 (95% CI 0.681 - 0.809). For MRI modality, T2 weighted image (T2WI) showed higher sensitivity (0.745) than T2WI plus diffusion weighted image (DWI) (0.697), while T2WI+DWI had higher specificity (0.809 vs 0.737) and AUC (0.829 vs 0.796), with no significant subgroup differences (p[&ge;]0.192). Models combining radiomics with clinical/radiological variables achieved significantly higher sensitivity (0.842 vs 0.657, p<0.001) and higher AUC (0.827 vs 0.752), with comparable specificity. Conclusion: MRI-based radiomics demonstrates good accuracy for preoperative detection of tumor deposits in RC. The integration of radiomics with clinical or imaging features increases the accuracy of identification. Amongst the MRI modalities, models that utilize the combination of T2WI plus DWI are more accurate than those using T2WI or DWI only.

Micro-fragmented adipose tissue reprogrammes the systemic molecular landscape of inflammatory knee osteoarthritis

Primorac, D.; Molnar, V.; Brlek, P.; Bulic, L.; Jelec, Z.; Prosenc Zmrzljak, U.; Klaric, T.; Lauc, G.; Malod-Dognin, N.; Przulj, N.

Knee osteoarthritis affects an estimated 374 million people and has no approved disease-modifying treatment. In our double-blind randomised trial (ISRCTN88966184), intra-articular micro-fragmented adipose tissue (MFAT) outperformed hyaluronic acid (HA) on pain, function and cartilage composition -- but clinical endpoints alone cannot say whether the disease changed or only the symptoms. Here we answer that question from the blood. Using an interpretable AI data-fusion framework based on non-negative matrix tri-factorisation, we integrated longitudinal plasma proteomics, N-glycomics and miRNA transcriptomics with patient genetics and prior molecular networks at baseline, one and six months, resolving 93 gene and 30 miRNA pathways, almost all significantly enriched in Gene Ontology Biological Process and Reactome annotations. By six months the two injections had left sharply distinct molecular fingerprints. HA remained dominated by canonical OA pathogenesis, including the cartilage-degrading effectors MMP13 and LIMK2 and persistent markers of synovial inflammation. MFAT instead shifted the systemic landscape towards chondroprotection, anti-inflammatory signalling and bone-cartilage homeostasis, prioritising effectors including SIRT7 and NDUFC1. These are, to our knowledge, the first in vivo human data to explain why a regenerative therapy outperforms viscosupplementation in knee osteoarthritis, and they place MFAT on the disease-modifying rather than the symptomatic side of that divide.

Nowroozi, A.; Bondarenko, M.; Brar, S. S.; Haas, B.; Brown, J.; Rustagi, A.; Graham, B.; Hudson, M.; Su, G.; Yala, A.; Sohn, J. H.

Objective: We aimed to 1) evaluate whether AI algorithms could identify a very low-risk sub-population Lung-RADS 1/2 cases suitable for a safe switch to biennial screening, and 2) externally validate Sybil, an imaging-based deep learning lung cancer risk prediction tool. Materials and methods: We gathered all lung cancer screenings (LCSs) from a tertiary safety-net hospital (n=3120, up to September 2024), manually extracted relevant clinical data, and analyzed Sybil's performance across various subgroups, with a specific focus on Lung-RADS 1 and 2 cases. The Brock model demonstrated an AUC of 0.513 on our data, with a sensitivity of 18.2% using the 1.5% threshold. Conclusion: Sybil has the potential to safely extend LCS intervals for low-risk individuals, reducing radiology workload and opening spots for life-saving new screenings.

BackgroundMalignant effusions are clinically accessible yet underutilized sources of metastatic tumor cells. However, abundant background immune cells complicate tumor cell isolation and molecular characterization. We integrate AI-powered single-cell imaging and morphology-guided label-free tumor cell enrichment with genomic profiling to characterize phenotypic and genomic heterogeneity in malignant effusions. MethodsWe applied a pre-trained deep learning model to brightfield images of single cells from malignant effusions of 10 patients with metastatic breast cancer to extract morphological features, quantify tumor cell fraction (TCF), and guide label-free microfluidic enrichment. ConclusionAI-powered analysis leverages single-cell morphology for tumor cell identification and label-free enrichment, while integration with genomic profiling enables simultaneous interrogation of phenotypic and genomic heterogeneity in malignant effusions. This approach expands the potential of malignant effusions for liquid biopsy applications and biomarker development and discovery.

Current gaps in downstream applications of fMRI foundation models: lessons learned from BrainLM & BrainHarmonix on Prevent-AD

Wang, H.-T.; Pichet Binette, A.; Sainath, P.; Farrugia, N.; Villeneuve, S.; Bellec, L.

Foundation models have driven major advancements in the language and vision domains, as seen in the popularity of Artificial Intelligence (AI) tools, and a growing number of studies are bringing the approach to human brain imaging. Whether these models are ready for independent researchers to use on clinical datasets has yet to be examined. We evaluated two recent fMRI foundation models, BrainLM and BrainHarmonix, on PreventAD, a dataset on adults with a family history of Alzheimers disease. We compared features from frozen and fine-tuned models against two baselines: functional connectivity and a dummy classifier that always predicts the most common class. The sample includes 343 subjects who passed quality control, with a subset of 218 who had amyloid PET imaging. We tested four prediction targets: sex, age, progression to mild cognitive impairment (MCI), and {beta}-amyloid burden. Both models required substantial changes to the released code. BrainLMs own data preparation workflow did not produce stable fine-tuning, which we achieved only after switching to a standard workflow implemented with the library Nilearn. BrainHarmonixs documentation did not specify how to normalize new data, so we resolved this by testing different alternatives. For sex, functional connectivity outperformed all foundation model features. For age group, the best foundation model feature, taken from BrainHarmonixs structural (T1-weighted) encoder, slightly exceeded functional connectivity, but the difference was not significant. For MCI and {beta}-amyloid, no feature exceeded the dummy classifier in accuracy, and the area under the curve (AUC) stayed near chance for all features. For both clinical targets, the most precise feature came from fine-tuned BrainLM, meaning a larger share of its positive predictions were correct than for functional connectivity, but neither difference reached statistical significance. These results suggested that the strong transfer to new tasks seen in large language models cannot yet be assumed for fMRI foundation models, and that both models required code adaptation that would preclude their streamlined adoption by independent groups. We recommend that future studies provide standardized data workflows and always report functional connectivity and dummy classifier baselines. For clinical targets with strong class imbalance, studies should report several metrics, including precision.

A Knowledge Database of Physical Activity Interventions for Hypertension using Large Language Models

Li, C.; Obioha, C.; Cui, X.; Zhou, X.; Rosenzweig, A.; Wang, M.

Physical exercise lowers blood pressure, but guidelines provide broad recommendations that rarely account for age, comorbidities, medications and hypertension severity. A structured, queryable resource of exercise interventions for hypertension is lacking, while manual curation is labor-intensive and difficult to scale. We present PAHD (Physical Activity for Hypertension Database), built with large language models (LLMs). An LLM screened 2,548 PubMed records through title-and-abstract and full-text assessment, selecting 255 studies. LLMs extracted arms, exercise protocols, participant characteristics, and outcomes, with two LLMs cross-checking study structure and each value linked to a verbatim quote. Programmatic integration generated 17,273 standardized records from 253 studies, one per reported comparison. Against adjudicated benchmarks, screening achieved 86.3% recall and 80.6% precision, and extraction 90.1% recall and 90.5% precision for blood pressure values. A clinician-facing portal retrieves studies matched to patients hypertension stage and comorbidities. PAHD is a reusable, traceable evidence resource for personalized exercise interventions in hypertension.
