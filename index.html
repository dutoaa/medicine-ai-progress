ClinVerse: A Realistic Multi-Agent Diagnostic Environment for Training and Evaluating Medical Agents

Sirry Chen, Fangke Chen, Xuanwen Ding, Wei Chen, Zhongyu Wei

Disease diagnosis is a key application of large language model (LLM) agents in healthcare. However, existing medical agents are primarily trained and evaluated with static question-answering data, providing limited opportunities to learn active information acquisition, examination decision-making, and dynamic clinical interaction. To address this limitation, we present ClinVerse, an interactive, real-data-grounded multi-agent environment designed for training and evaluating medical diagnostic agents. Built on ClinPatient, a scalable dataset comprising 150k diagnostic cases derived from real-world electronic health records, ClinVerse supports coordinated interactions among six specialized clinical roles. Specifically, a doctor agent starts from an initial patient complaint, progressively gathers clinical evidence through patient interviews and examination requests, and ultimately produces a diagnosis. For each interaction trajectory, a rule-based scoring framework quantifies information acquisition, examination ordering, and diagnostic accuracy. These trajectory-level scores serve as reward signals for agent training while also enabling scalable evaluation. In experiments, we evaluate various general-purpose and medical-domain models and show that nine self-evolution methods across three categories consistently improve agents' diagnostic capabilities within ClinVerse. We further analyze the interplay among model capability, environment feedback, and self-evolution methods.

DocTalk: Enhancing Medical Knowledge Retrieval via Fine-tuned Large Language Models with Chain-of-Thought Reasoning for Robust Clinical Decision Support

Asad Gohar, Md Ariful Islam Mozumder, Moon-Il Joo, Hee-Cheol Kim

Objective: The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) in healthcare has opened new possibilities for AI-assisted clinical decision-making; however, safe and effective deployment remains challenging because of hallucination and domain misalignment. This study introduces DocTalk, a doctor-assistive question-answering (QA) system designed to generate concise, chart-grounded responses from de-identified discharge summaries. Methods: We developed a high-fidelity data generation pipeline that produces 500 clinically relevant QA pairs through sentence-level decomposition, chain-of-thought-guided question formulation, and expert review. Six open-source LLMs, including three general-purpose and three biomedical models, were evaluated before and after parameter-efficient instruction tuning using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). The framework integrates prompt engineering, in-context learning, and medically aligned evaluation to support factual and interpretable responses. Results: On DocTalk-QA, instruction tuning produced consistent improvements across lexical, semantic, and clinical fidelity metrics. The best-performing tuned model, Llama-3-Med42-8B, achieved ROUGE-L = 0.40, BERTScore-F1 = 0.76, and UMLS-F1 = 0.49. To examine external generalization beyond the DocTalk-QA dataset, the two best-performing models, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Llama-3-Med42-8B, were evaluated on PubMedQA before and after instruction tuning. Accuracy improved from 0.931 to 0.977 for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and from 0.935 to 0.943 for Llama-3-Med42-8B. Conclusion: DocTalk was deployed as a secure web-based platform for interactive EHR querying. These findings suggest that clinically grounded instruction tuning can improve medical QA performance and support practical clinical decision support.

Wei-Wei Zhang, Pengkai Wang, Yan Li, Zhijie Sang, Guan-Qun Zhou, Jun-Yan Li

Large language models (LLMs) show promise in specialty medicine, yet existing evaluations largely focus on isolated clinical encounters rather than tracking how clinical responsibilities evolve across a longitudinal care pathway. Here we introduce NPC-RubricBench, a phase-aware benchmark for evaluating multistage clinical reasoning across the cancer care continuum, using nasopharyngeal carcinoma as a defined disease context. Curated from longitudinal records of 405 patients across two cancer centers, the benchmark comprises 941 patient-phase units across six sequential care phases and 17,688 patient-specific atomic criteria spanning five evaluation dimensions, with each assessment strictly restricted to decision-time clinical evidence. Evaluating 30 general-purpose and medical LLMs revealed that strong diagnostic performance does not carry over to downstream care. We evaluated 30 general-purpose and medical LLMs, showing that high diagnostic performance did not translate into improved downstream care. Instead, the dominant failure modes reversed across phases. Explicit decision errors dominated anatomical staging at 48.2%, whereas decision omissions dominated initial work-up and post-treatment follow-up at 33.4% and 30.4%, respectively. In the evaluated model set, medically adapted models showed higher omission rates than general-purpose models. Moreover, varying query granularity exposes a critical specification trade-off: broad open-ended requests degrade Decision Quality across all 30 models, while more detailed, checklist-like prompts raise Decision Quality but reduce Evidence Faithfulness and Safety Adherence. Furthermore, NPC-RubricBench demonstrates that static diagnostic benchmarks are insufficient to ensure consistent clinical reliability over time, highlighting the importance of phase-adaptive safeguards in multistage medical AI.

Nadun Rajapaksha, Sandun Herath, Devnith Wijesinghe, Isiri Amani Withanawasam, Hasindri Watawana, Ranga Rodrigo

Whole Slide Image (WSI) analysis serves as the fundamental method for the clinical diagnosis of cancer in digital histopathology. Recent advances in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have enabled state-of-the-art (SOTA) automated histopathology report generation. However, MLLMs reason purely through distributions encoded in their neural parameters, limiting the amount of knowledge and reasoning logic they can contain given the model scale. Their black-box reasoning poses another challenge to the human interpretation of intermediate reasoning. We address these issues by utilizing knowledge graphs to inject external structured knowledge for MLLMs to reason over, while making this reasoning component visible. Knowledge graphs are capable of partially externalizing the containment of medical knowledge, an area that remains underexplored in pathology report generation. We introduce PathKGen, a knowledge graph-assisted MLLM for histopathology report generation. PathKGen has two main branches: (1) a visual path that learns visual token inputs to the MLLM using pathology foundation models, and (2) a knowledge path that learns knowledge token inputs by extracting key morphological features of WSIs through querying a pathology-specific knowledge graph. A comprehensive evaluation on WSI-Bench dataset shows that PathKGen surpasses previous SOTA methods while revealing diagnostic reasoning as visually intuitive graphs. Due to its modular design, the proposed knowledge path is complementary to vision-aligned MLLMs and can be extended to other applications by curating domain-specific knowledge graphs to generate knowledge tokens.

Beyond Federated Learning: Decentralized Agentic Systems for Self-Evolving Clinical Intelligence

Haoyuan Li, Mathias Funk, Aaqib Saeed

Clinical expertise is inherently distributed: it resides not in any single dataset or institution but in the accumulated reasoning experiences of thousands of clinicians navigating diverse patient populations, resource constraints, and evolving treatment protocols. Yet current approaches to scaling medical AI, from centralized large language models to federated learning, remain anchored to a data-centric paradigm that treats knowledge as static parameters to be aggregated. In this Perspective, we argue for a fundamental shift from data aggregation to experience aggregation. We propose Decentralized Agentic Systems (DAS), a framework in which distributed AI agents operating across healthcare institutions synthesize collective clinical intelligence by sharing abstracted reasoning trajectories rather than raw data or model parameters. Unlike federated learning, which averages numerical gradients across homogeneous model architectures, DAS exchanges structured records of clinical reasoning, specifically the sequence of observations, hypotheses, tool invocations, and outcome feedback that constitute diagnostic experience. We examine the architectural principles, deployment considerations, governance requirements, and critical open challenges of this framework, laying the groundwork for a collaborative medical AI ecosystem where distributed expertise autonomously adapts to the complexities of longitudinal patient care.

Acute Stroke Classification Using Asymmetric Data Standardization and Orthogonal-Focal Loss Optimized CNN-ViT Cross-Attention Fusion: A Multicenter External Validation Study

Tansel Uyar

Background/Objectives: Rapid differentiation of ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke subtypes is vital for acute management. Although current deep learning models achieve high single-dataset accuracy, they suffer from "shortcut learning" and domain shift in external validation. This study proposes a hierarchical computer-aided diagnostic (CAD) system mimicking emergency triage. Methods: We introduce a 3-channel Radiology-Focused Preprocessing pipeline that feeds radiological Hounsfield Unit (HU) windows into Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) and Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones via asymmetric formats (.mat and .png). The architecture utilizes a novel joint cross-attention fusion network optimized by an Integrated Orthogonal-Focal Loss. Results: Our system achieved a 95.48% F1-score on the internal stroke dataset. However, multi-center external validation (AISD, BHSD, PhysioNet) using a Mask Anchor filter revealed a drop to the 58-62% range, exposing the "internal validation illusion" caused by hardware noise. Critically, models exhibited high precision (77%) but low sensitivity (57%) for ischemic stroke, proving prone to hemorrhage overdiagnosis. Multi-seed variance analysis demonstrated that the DenseNet201+ViT backbone yielded the lowest deviation (0.59) and highest stability, making it the most reliable architecture for real-world clinical integration. Conclusions: To overcome diagnostic challenges, future research must focus on multimodal architectures that combine 3D volumetric extraction with clinical data.

Evaluating Bayesian deep learning for robust biomedical time-series classification under distribution shift: A sleep scoring case study

Mads Birch Sørensen, Ulrike Richter, Francois Gastambide, Michael Riis Andersen

In biomedical time-series classification, machine learning models often suffer from deteriorating accuracy and calibration due to distribution shift. We investigate to what degree Bayesian-inspired deep learning improves robustness through Bayesian model averaging (BMA). Using rodent sleep scoring as a case study, we evaluate BMA under distribution shifts in genotype, laboratory, and species. We systematically compare nine approximate inference methods spanning single- and multi-basin approaches across two neural network backbones on in-distribution (ID) data and five out-of-distribution (OOD) datasets. In our results, multi-basin methods perform best, outperforming baselines and single-basin methods. Compared with point estimates, BMA generally improves OOD accuracy and calibration across both architectures, while yielding only modest ID gains. It also often improves selective prediction and OOD and artifact detection, but, interestingly, not inter-rater disagreement detection.

The evaluation results highlight both the potential of these approaches and the remaining challenges in patient attribution and phenotype normalization from full-text biomedical literature. This work improves the interpretability of temporal graph-based models in longitudinal, team-based healthcare, with potential applicability to other evolving collaborative settings.

TotalSegmentator-based nnU-Net models delineate the liver, Couinaud segments, and lesions, while computational tools extract volumes, dimensions, counts, and anatomical locations. By connecting quantitative imaging evidence with agentic report generation, this work takes a step toward human-centered medical AI that prioritizes traceability and clinical relevance.

A Visual Grounding Benchmark for Retinal OCT with Multimodal Large Language Models

Ronald Fecso, José Morano, Botond Fazekas, Paul Widmann-Sedlnitzky, Dorota Himel, Laetita Hinterhuber

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can answer clinical questions about medical images, but trustworthy use requires visual grounding: models should localize the pathology they report rather than rely on spurious shortcuts. Whether MLLMs can visually ground clinical findings in retinal optical coherence tomography (OCT), the most widely used imaging modality in ophthalmology, remains largely unexplored. To address this gap, we introduce RetinaGroundBench, the first benchmark for biomarker visual grounding in retinal OCT. It comprises 815 B-scans exhaustively annotated by ophthalmologists with 4,537 bounding boxes covering 13 biomarkers across 8 diseases. Because MLLMs often fragment a single lesion into multiple partial predictions, we propose AggIoU, a metric that evaluates whether the union of overlapping predicted boxes covers the annotated region. Across 27 open-source MLLMs (0.8-38B parameters), we find that visual grounding remains largely unsolved: the best general-purpose model achieves a macro F1 of just 23.7%, with consistently low recall, despite reaching up to 73% balanced accuracy on biomarker presence questions. To narrow this gap, we developed RetGrounder, an MLLM fine-tuned using an OCT-specific strategy integrating grounding and textual information. Despite its relatively small size (8B), RetGrounder improves grounding F1 to 33.9%, a 43% relative improvement. Our benchmark provides a reference point for measuring progress in the field of OCT diagnosis with MLLMs.
