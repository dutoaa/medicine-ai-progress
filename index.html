Overview of CHIP 2026 Shared Task 1: Patient-Specific Phenotype Concept Recognition in Full-Text Biomedical Literature

Xuanlyu Zhu, Feng Tian, Yang Zhong, Pengqi Zheng, Xiwen Bi, Ling Luo

Automatic phenotype information extraction plays an important role in rare disease diagnosis, genotype-phenotype analysis, and precision medicine. However, accurately identifying phenotype concepts and determining their associations with specific patients remain challenging, particularly in full-text biomedical articles involving multiple patients and complex clinical descriptions. This paper presents an overview of CHIP 2026 Shared Task 1, which aims to promote the development of methods for patient-specific phenotype concept recognition from full-text biomedical literature. The task consists of two subtasks: full-text phenotype concept recognition and patient-specific phenotype association. For this evaluation, we used 200 annotated full-text biomedical articles from the PatientPheX dataset, containing 506 patients, 15,193 phenotype mentions, and 5,656 patient-phenotype associations. A total of 455 teams registered for the competition, with 189 and 103 valid submissions received on the Phase A and Phase B leaderboards, respectively. The best-performing system achieved an overall score of 0.7389 on the final test set, outperforming the baseline by 8.37 percentage points. The top-performing teams explored diverse approaches combining biomedical language models, large language models (LLMs), and ensemble strategies. The evaluation results highlight both the potential of these approaches and the remaining challenges in patient attribution and phenotype normalization from full-text biomedical literature. The post-evaluation phase remains open at https://tianchi.aliyun.com/competition/entrance/532512.

ViBMR: A Large-Scale Multi-Sequence Brain MRI Dataset and Comprehensive Evaluation for Vietnamese Radiology Report Generation

Dai Lam Le, Minh Khoi Nguyen, Son Dinh Nguyen, Amir Reza Jafari, Viet Ha, Nguyen Son

Automated radiology report generation for multi-sequence brain MRI holds strong promise for alleviating radiologists' workload, yet it remains a demanding problem: brain MRI studies are inherently volumetric, each pulse sequence carries complementary diagnostic information, and annotated data are critically scarce for low-resource clinical languages. Existing brain MRI-report resources are still dominated by English reports, translated clinical text, or datasets adapted from task-specific imaging benchmarks, leaving routine low-resource clinical reporting and heterogeneous multi-sequence MRI protocols insufficiently represented. To bridge this gap, we introduce ViBMR, to the best of our knowledge, the first large-scale multi-sequence brain MRI dataset paired with routine Vietnamese clinical reports, comprising 3,649 patients and 11,875 MRI series across T1, T2, FLAIR, and contrast-enhanced T1 sequences. Building on ViBMR, we establish a comprehensive evaluation setup for brain MRI report generation, covering representative medical vision-language models, multi-sequence inputs, and feature aggregation strategies. The results provide key insights into dataset characteristics and model behavior, while demonstrating the necessity of native low-resource clinical datasets for reliable Vietnamese-origin brain MRI report generation. Our dataset and baseline models will be released for non-commercial research.

From Clinical Risk Prediction to Operational Decisions: An Uncertainty-Aware Framework for ICU Staffing

Jiayan Yang, Ad Feelders, Arno Siebes

Advances in electronic health records (EHR) and large-scale critical care databases have enabled the development of machine learning models for the early detection of patient deterioration. While these models often achieve strong predictive performance for outcomes such as sepsis and mortality, most studies evaluate them purely on discrimination metrics. This leaves a critical gap: how to systematically translate probabilistic clinical risk estimates into downstream operational decisions under real-world constraints, such as shift rigidity and hiring delays. This study proposes an uncertainty-aware anticipatory staffing framework that bridges short-term patient risk prediction and staff planning without altering the upstream predictive model. Instead of relying on naive deterministic aggregation or fixed safety buffers, the framework explicitly models dynamic predictive uncertainty and evaluates extreme shortage exposure using Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR). These aggregated, risk-calibrated demand signals are then embedded into an hourly rolling-horizon scheduling policy to generate delay-constrained staffing decisions. Evaluated on realistic trajectories from the MIMIC-IV database, the proposed framework demonstrates a favorable cost-shortage trade-off compared to deterministic and purely reactive baselines. Pareto frontier analysis illustrates how uncertainty-aware demand construction changes resource allocation under varying safety preferences, while stress testing confirms the framework's operational robustness even when subjected to systematic predictive bias in the form of calibration drift. By formalizing this translation layer, this work provides a principled, transparent pathway for safely integrating probabilistic AI outputs into high-stakes healthcare operational systems.

Expandable Modality Experts for Medical Vision-Language Learning

DM BAPPY, Eunsu Kim, Hanjoo Lee, Sung Hak Lee, Sangjeong Ahn

Learning across medical imaging modalities requires making use of images without paired text and accommodating new domains while retaining existing representations. We propose a medical vision-language technique built around an expandable bank of modality experts. Its Multiway Transformer combines shared visual computation with deterministically routed feed-forward experts and a separate text pathway. A modality-aware visual tokenizer supplies targets for masked pretraining on 19.4 million unpaired image rows across seven modalities; contrastive alignment then uses 3.23 million image-text pairs. Additional fundus and optical coherence tomography experts learn from paired data while the original image and text embedding paths remain frozen. With a ViT-Large backbone, the proposed technique achieves the best or tied-best result in 24 of 25 benchmark settings across nine modalities: 10 of 11 zero-shot classification benchmarks, all 10 linear probes, and all four retrieval benchmarks. Mean balanced accuracy is 0.674 for zero-shot classification and 0.856 for linear probing, and mean bidirectional retrieval recall is 20.70%. A separate three-seed sequential experiment with matched adaptation data yields fundus and OCT retrieval gains of 14.95 and 9.31 percentage points, with no measured loss on preceding fundus or pathology tasks. The preservation argument requires unchanged existing computation paths and inference conditions, and feature-identity checks verify retention on evaluated inputs. The proposed technique provides a modular approach to learning and expanding medical vision-language representations.

Are Joint-Embedding Predictive Objectives Effective for Longitudinal EHRs?

Ashvin Gupta, Brendan Delaney, Alessandra Russo

Masked language modelling (MLM) is the dominant pretraining objective for transformer encoders applied to longitudinal electronic health records (EHRs). However, reconstructing individual events may overemphasise noisy or weakly informative observations and does not explicitly encourage learning higher-level patient states. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) instead learn by predicting representations in latent space and have shown strong performance in a variety of domains. In this paper, we investigate to what extent JEPA can be adapted to EHR sequence representation. Our empirical investigation using MIMIC-IV shows that although all three of our JEPA formulations train stably without representational collapse, they consistently underperform standard MLM. We find that target size, not target heterogeneity, increases prediction error, yet reducing the target size does not recover downstream performance. This suggests that stable latent prediction alone is insufficient for competitive EHR representation learning and highlights the challenge of defining clinically meaningful latent targets for EHR.

Temporal Evidence Allocation For Longitudinal Clinical Retrieval: A Position And Technical Report

Soumya Ranjan Sahoo, Divya Bharti, Sanand Sasidharan

Electronic health records are written to support care, not retrieval. Across years of oncology notes, the primary diagnosis is restated in report after report, while the events that shape a patient's course, such as a recurrence or a change in therapy, may be recorded only once. A retriever that ranks passages by similarity to the query keeps returning the repeated material and can miss these transitions, that is, it exhibits temporal redundancy across historical narratives. We argue that longitudinal clinical retrieval should be treated as allocating a limited evidence budget across time, not just ranking by relevance. We separate the task into three objectives: temporal evidence allocation, candidate reconstruction, and clinical validation. In Study A (13 clinical queries over 20 patient records, with two-stage reconstruction), the Temporal-15 configuration reaches a milestone slot recall of 0.538, compared with 0.388 for the Global-50 baseline. However, it also retrieves far more evidence (496 versus 50 indexed units per query), so the gain cannot be credited to allocation alone. When evidence is held fixed, removing prompted precedence rules raises schema-order reversals from 0.010 to 0.080. A five-patient, single-query pilot with fixed budgets finds that wider temporal coverage does not reliably yield more dated milestones. Together, these results suggest that how much a record says about a period is a poor guide to how useful that period is as evidence. Because no expert reference timelines are available, our measurements describe retrieval behavior rather than clinical accuracy. This position and technical report presents the retrieval analysis behind the Onco-CONSTRUE framework and sets out a research agenda for budget-constrained longitudinal clinical retrieval.

A general-purpose System One decision model for clinical records

Andrew Zhang, Trinh T. L. Vuong, Sophia J. Wagner, Dongmin Bang, Tong Ding, Yuchang Su

Clinical care, health-system operations and biomedical discovery depend on extracting reliable answers from patient records, yet applying diverse clinical questions across entire populations remains computationally expensive. Here we present Clinical System One (CS1), a 4-billion-parameter decision model for fast, typed judgments from clinical records, along with two agentic harnesses in which CS1 works together with a generalist language model. CS1-4B is trained on 12.4 million questions and answers from 3.6 million patients across a multi-hospital health system, combining structured-data keys, observed outcomes and language-model supervision across clinical notes, diagnostic reports and patient timelines. It answers multiple typed questions in a single forward pass without task-specific retraining, and each answer carries a probability that can route uncertain cases to human review. On 963,408 held-out patients, CS1-4B outperformed open decision models up to 27 billion parameters on 13 of 14 tasks scored independently of its training teacher. Across 21 external data sets, its aggregate performance approached that of the strongest tested 27-billion-parameter model, with 6.4-fold faster inference in a matched workload. Integrating CS1-4B with a language model agent produced cited answers over complete multi-year patient histories and screened a patient population against the eligibility criteria of 17 registered clinical trials, placing four times as many judged-eligible patients among its top 20 candidates as keyword search while using 690-fold fewer language-model tokens than a language model reading every record. These findings support a division of labour in clinical AI, with small decision models performing repeated record reading and generative models formulating questions and synthesizing evidence.

Africa-Papers: A 10-Billion-Token Multi-Domain Academic Corpus for African Foundation Language Models

Mich-Seth Owusu

Large Language Models (LLMs) depend fundamentally on the diversity, depth, and factual rigor of their pre-training corpora. However, contemporary foundational pre-training datasets (such as The Pile, S2ORC, and Dolma) exhibit a severe structural omission: African scientific, medical, legal, and agricultural scholarship constitutes less than 0.5% of global pre-training tokens. Consequently, state-of-the-art models suffer from systematic failure modes when reasoning about tropical epidemiology, localized smallholder agro-ecologies, regional economic governance, and indigenous linguistic structures. To resolve this representation deficit and advance African intellectual sovereignty in artificial intelligence, we introduce Africa-Papers, the largest curated multi-domain Pan-African academic pre-training corpus to date. Comprising over 844,807 full-text papers spanning 10.15 billion tokens across 879 Parquet shards, Africa-Papers unifies four primary scientific streams: (1) continental peer-reviewed journals indexed in DOAJ across 31 African nations, (2) the CGIAR CGSpace agricultural repository, (3) African-authored and African-focused STEM/AI preprints on arXiv, and (4) open-access international publications by scholars affiliated with African institutions indexed via OpenAlex. The entire corpus is processed into layout-aware, clean Markdown preserving section hierarchies, mathematical equations, and tabular data, accompanied by exact BPE token counts and a standardized 8-domain taxonomy. We release Africa-Papers openly on the Hugging Face Hub under the AfriSpeech organization to accelerate continual pre-training and domain evaluation for foundation models globally.

Discrete Neural Interlingua for Ontology-Agnostic EHR Modeling

Tong Ding, Caiwei Tian, Dongmin Bang, Ming Y. Lu, Sophia J. Wagner, Andrew Zhang

EHR foundation models promise scalable clinical prediction across health systems, yet their deployment is constrained by a tokenization bottleneck. The same clinical concept can appear as different ontology codes, local identifiers, synonyms, or free-text descriptions, which standard EHR tokenizers treat as unrelated symbols. A model trained on MIMIC-III with a closed-vocabulary tokenizer can use only 13% of eICU events. We introduce MEDIATOR, a discrete ontology-agnostic tokenizer that maps any clinical concept expressed as text into a shared vector-quantized codebook, turning EHR harmonization into open-vocabulary tokenization without manual mappings or a fixed vocabulary. Trained with synonym supervision and guided by enriched concept descriptions, code co-occurrence, and relation graphs, MEDIATOR collapses equivalent expressions while organizing distinct concepts in a medically meaningful discrete space. Used as the token interface of an EHR Transformer, MEDIATOR improves mean AUPRC over the strongest prior tokenizer by 4.0 points (8% relative) on 20 MIMIC-III tasks and by 8.6 points (29% relative) in zero-shot transfer to 14 eICU tasks, where it uses all events. The transfer gain combines coverage and representation, as MEDIATOR remains the best tokenizer under identical inputs (+1.0 and +1.9 points on operational and subcohort-mortality tasks). Same-encoder controls show that the text encoder drives in-domain accuracy, while the learned objectives of MEDIATOR drive transfer robustness and entity-linking quality. MEDIATOR also enables transferable attribution across hospitals.

Block Diffusion for Longitudinal EHR: Accuracy, Efficiency, and Representation Trade-offs

Ashvin Gupta, Joshua Placidi, Brendan Delaney, Alessandra Russo

Autoregressive (AR) models have proved effective for zero-shot prediction from longitudinal electronic health records, but impose strictly sequential generation on trajectories containing temporally clustered and interdependent clinical events. We investigate block-based masked diffusion (BD) as an alternative on MIMIC-IV under matched tokenisation, context length, model capacity, and evaluation conditions. Across block sizes, BD exhibits a clear quality--efficiency trade-off, with small-block configurations retaining much of the predictive performance of AR while enabling substantially faster inference. A representative $B{=}8$ configuration achieves approximately $1.43\times$ faster evaluation. Although generative rollout remains below AR in most settings, frozen linear probes on BD and AR representations achieve near-identical performance across clinical prediction tasks, indicating that BD learns comparably informative clinical representations. Increasing the denoising budget yields diminishing returns, while alternative decoding strategies provide limited gains. Overall, our results show that block diffusion is a viable and computationally attractive approach to longitudinal EHR modelling, with further improvements in iterative decoding offering a promising route toward closing the remaining generative performance gap.
