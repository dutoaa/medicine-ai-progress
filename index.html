Findings-aware Language-Image Pretraining for CT Volume Interpretation

Takuro Shimaya, Junya Inoh, Masahiro Saiko

Computed tomography (CT) provides diverse patient information and various clinical tasks, for which numerous deep learning models have been investigated. Recent years have seen the development of vision-language foundation models that can comprehensively extract clinical features, driven by representation learning that combines CT volumes with radiology reports. Existing approaches typically adopt Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP)-based representation learning, where pairs from different cases are, in principle, treated as negative pairs such that the volume and report embeddings are pushed apart. In the biomedical imaging, however, treating different cases as negative pairs is not always appropriate, particularly when clinical similarity exists. In this study, we propose a supervised representation learning framework grounded in clinical knowledge from language foundation models, rather than the weak supervision used in CLIP. Specifically, we utilized a large language model to extract abnormal finding phrases from reports. We then explicitly constructed the target features that the image encoder should output, as the semantic representation of the extracted findings using a biomedical text embedding model. Our Findings-aware Language-Image Pre-training (FiLIP) outperformed weakly supervised approaches in multi-label zero-shot finding classification in both in-domain and external-domain evaluations, and also proved effective when fine-tuned for the downstream task of radiology report generation.

Multidimensional Suicide Risk Assessment with Machine Learning using Electronic Health Records

Nathan Scollar, Kemal Davaslioglu, Nathan Conger, Sastry Kompella, Yalin E. Sagduyu, Nathaniel D. Bastian

Suicide prevention remains a critical public health challenge, and electronic health records (EHRs) provide an opportunity to characterize suicide-related risk indicators using documented clinical, behavioral, medical, and social-contextual information. This paper presents a multidimensional machine learning framework to model and estimate suicide-related outcomes using MIMIC-IV EHR data. We construct clinically motivated diagnosis categories and define a composite outcome from documented codes corresponding to suicidal ideation, suicide attempt, prior suicidal behavior, and intentional self-harm. To address severe class imbalance and reduce age-related confounding, we apply age-matched resampling that retains all positive patients and samples comparison-cohort patients from the same age group. Logistic regression, random forest, and XGBoost classifiers are evaluated before and after resampling with isotonic calibration. The calibrated XGBoost model achieves the strongest matched-cohort performance, outperforming the other evaluated classifiers. SHAP-based interpretation shows that diagnostic burden, depression-related features, age, personality-disorder history, social determinants related to violence, and medical comorbidity contribute to elevated predicted probabilities. Our results demonstrate that combining broad EHR-derived features, age-matched cohort construction, calibration, and feature-level interpretation provides a practical framework for retrospective suicide-related risk modeling.

MIMIC-Note-Extract: A Large-Scale Structured Dataset Extracted from MIMIC-IV-Note Discharge Summaries

Kristian Schwethelm, Jonas Kuntzer, Conrad Ketzer, Eva Wende, Moritz Schlager, Daniel Rueckert

MIMIC is one of the most widely used public hospital datasets for machine learning on electronic health records, yet clinically important information, such as past medical history, allergies, and diagnostic findings, remains confined to free-text discharge summaries. Studies requiring this information must therefore build their own extraction pipelines, duplicating substantial effort across projects. We release MIMIC-Note-Extract (MNE), a large-scale dataset that uses a hybrid extraction pipeline combining deterministic parsing with schema-guided large language model generation to transform 16 sections from 300,720 discharge summaries into structured tables. MNE adds information that has no structured MIMIC counterpart and enriches related tables with broader coverage and greater clinical detail, including 10 times more patient-history entries and medication histories for more than twice as many admissions. Validation by medical doctors confirms extraction accuracy above 92% across sections. Using MNE, we construct a clinical decision-making benchmark for LLM agents, demonstrating how reusable structured representations of clinical narratives reduce data curation effort.

From Natural Images to Medical Scans: Transferability of Diffusion Models for Medical Image Generation

Efthymios Georgiou, Ioannis Kakogeorgiou, Sarah Brüningk

Transfer learning from natural images is standard in supervised and increasingly adopted in self-supervised medical image analysis, but its role in generative modelling, and in particular diffusion denoisers, remains underexplored. Medical diffusion models typically train the denoiser from scratch, with no systematic evaluation of natural-image transfer. We analyse this across latent and pixel paradigms, model sizes (131M-952M) and two radiology datasets (RadImageNet, BraTS), and ask which aspects of transfer support generation. We find that: i) Natural-image initialisation consistently improves generation, by 21-34% relative gFID in pixel space against 14% in latent space, where the tokeniser supplies much of the prior. gFID gains mirror downstream pathology classification and radiomic-feature FD, both proxies for clinical fidelity. ii) Benefits reflect spatial structure, not semantics: semantic probing does not predict generation quality, whereas a spatial-structure metric adapted to internal denoiser representations tracks gFID at $|r|=0.85$, positioning spatial organisation as a channel through which pretraining manifests. iii) Mixed-domain denoiser training shows that natural and medical images share similar layer-wise representations up to the late blocks, and mixing natural images into medical training improves fidelity. iv) Transfer benefit is tied to natural image initialisation, outperforming in-domain medical pretraining and several reinitialisation schemes. Overall, natural image denoisers transfer positively to medical ones in our setup. Code is available at https://github.com/CAIROLab-Bern/rad-dit

Rethinking Next-Visit Diagnosis Prediction Beyond Aggregate Metrics: Retrieval--Memory Multi-Agent Adjudication

Heejoon Koo

Next-visit diagnosis prediction forecasts the diagnoses documented at a patient's next encounter, turning retrospective electronic health records into prospective clinical prediction. However, conventional aggregate evaluation obscures its multifaceted nature: distributionally, models must handle variable-size diagnosis sets and long-tailed labels; temporally, they must distinguish persistent, newly recorded, and recurrent diagnoses; and both vary with observed history depth. We introduce an evaluation framework along these dimensions and apply it to rule-based heuristics, LLM-based prompting, retrieval, memory, and multi-agent approaches. This reveals that repeating the last visit outperforms most LLM-based approaches overall despite predicting no new or recurrent diagnoses, and that retrieval and memory exhibit distinct profiles whose relative strengths vary across temporal transitions and backbones. Motivated by this, we propose LONGINUS, a retrieval-memory multi-agent adjudication framework combining evidence retrieved from patient records with structured memory of the patient trajectory. Using cross-view agreement as a reliability heuristic, it preserves shared predictions and adjudicates disagreements without revealing their source. Across MIMIC-III and MIMIC-IV with four LLM backbones, LONGINUS achieves the best transition-specific performance among LLM-based methods in nearly all settings while remaining competitive with them on aggregate metrics.

Trick-or-Treat: Adversarial Evaluation of Multimodal Medical Reasoning with Synthetic Images

Oishi Banerjee, Eujin Lee, Sung Eun Kim, Alexandra N. Willauer, Christine Y. Zhou, Zhizhou Yang

We used medical image synthesis to create a challenging multimodal evaluation framework that exposes hallucination behaviors in frontier models. Evaluating multimodal reasoning has historically been difficult because existing medical datasets rarely pair different image modalities, such as radiographs and external clinical photographs. To address this gap, we introduce Trick-or-Treat, an adversarial multimodal medical benchmark that pairs real chest radiographs with synthetic clinical photographs to challenge models' cross-modal reasoning. For each of a wide range of conditions, we created two pairs of images, where the pairs share a single chest radiograph but contain different external photographs. In matched pairs, the chest radiograph and photograph both contain findings suggesting the same disease (e.g., varicella pneumonia suggested by lung opacities and crusted skin lesions seen in chickenpox). In mismatched pairs, the photograph depicts an alternative clinical finding that would not lead to the same unifying diagnosis (e.g. replacing crusted skin lesions with ordinary acne). Synthetic photograph generation allows clinical findings to be varied while the person's features and photographic setting stay consistent, reducing confounding. In an investigation of three frontier models, we found that models hallucinated clinically significant photograph findings in 45-57.5% of mismatched pairs, up from 25-37.5% for the same photographs shown alone. When faced with mismatched pairs, models tended to ignore mismatches and instead hallucinated findings that better matched the radiograph (e.g. misreading acne as crusted skin lesions to justify diagnosing chickenpox). Trick-or-Treat both demonstrates concerning error pattern in frontier models' medical reasoning and provides a proof of concept for the use of synthetic images in adversarial multimodal evaluation.

Federated Transfer Learning for Medical Image Classification under Mixed Skew

Alexandre G. Leclercq, Sébastien Bougleux, Aurélien Corroyer-Dulmont, Romain Hérault

Deep learning in medical imaging is constrained by data scarcity and by privacy regulations that make centralizing patient data across institutions impractical. Federated Learning (FL) addresses these constraints by keeping data local, but its distributed nature exposes training to strong statistical heterogeneity. Multicentric medical data is particularly affected, exhibiting a mixed skew that combines feature, label, concept, and quantity shifts across centers. Personalized FL mitigates heterogeneity by tailoring a model to each client, yet most methods assume training from scratch, which is unrealistic given the high dimensionality of medical features relative to the limited data available. We propose the Personalized Aligner Federated Pretrained (PAFP) framework, a personalized FL approach built on a pre-trained backbone. PAFP inserts client-specific Feature Alignment modules before the fine-tuned blocks to realign hidden feature distributions toward a server-aggregated target. On the FedISIC benchmark, PAFP attains the highest unweighted balanced accuracy while reducing inter-client performance variance roughly six-fold relative to the strongest baseline, yielding accuracy and cross-client equity jointly. Our code is publicly available at https://github.com/Alexandre-Leclercq/PAFP.

Benchmark of THReD: Evaluating an LLM Agent and Its Preprocessing Tools for Turning Electronic Health Records into AI-Ready Datasets

Javad Mohammad Alizadeh, Huanmei Wu

LLM agents are increasingly built into data-analysis software, yet little evidence shows how reliably they preprocess clinical data or whether the tools they invoke give correct, reproducible output. We report a benchmark of THReD, a web-based system that turns electronic health record (EHR) extracts into AI-ready datasets through a chat agent whose proposed steps run only after user confirmation. The agent benchmark has 141 tasks (100 templates) in seven categories, including goal-level requests, failure recovery, requests calling for no action, schema variation and prompt injection through the data, on synthetic tables calibrated to a regional health information exchange; success is judged by checks on the produced data. Over 2,550 episodes with Claude Haiku 5.5 in four context configurations, the deployed agent completed 92.7% (95% CI 88.1-96.4) of tasks on its first answer, abstained on every advice request, recovered from every injected fault, and never proposed an action requested by planted text. Without the data profile, completion fell to 58.0%; goal-level requests were hardest (80.6%). Six experiments on real records of two chronic-disease cohorts and on synthetic data at scale validate the tools: 91-92% of inferred patient classes and 99.7-99.8% of inferred vital-sign labels were correct in masked reconstruction; a dictionary builder combining curated dictionaries with LLM suggestions mapped 99.0% of 2,208 raw variants correctly; all 2,176 replays of 128 saved pipelines gave byte-identical files under pinned dependencies; subgroup differences in inference accuracy vanished within facility and lookup key; and the same-day consolidation rule changed 90-day emergency revisit rates by up to 3.2 points without shifting cohort composition by more than one point. Processing time grew roughly linearly to 5.5 million records while the prompt stayed at about 13 thousand tokens.

MedPsy: State-of-the-Art Small Medical Language Models for Efficient Edge Deployment

Davide Vitabile, Alexandro Buffa, Akshay Nambiar, Amril Nazir

Medical language models promise expert clinical support, but the strongest open-source LLMs remain too large for private edge-device deployment, while compact medical models still trail substantially on knowledge-intensive and real-world healthcare tasks. Reasoning models that fit edge memory budgets can produce long reasoning traces, increasing sequential decoding, end-to-end latency, energy consumption, and KV-cache memory pressure. We present MedPsy, a family of text-only 1.7B and 4B medical language models designed for edge deployment, targeting this accuracy-generation-efficiency trade-off. Our recipe combines a synthetic medical data pipeline over biology, medicine, and health seeds with chain-of-thought targets from a 235B medical-focused LLM teacher; a four-stage post-training curriculum comprising two SFT stages followed by two RL stages with hard-sample mining; and an edge deployment evaluation spanning multiple GGUF quantization variants and real-device inference on laptop and smartphone hardware. On seven closed-ended medical benchmarks, MedPsy-4B surpasses MedGemma-1.5-4B by 19.34 points and matches MedGemma-27B while being 6.75x smaller; MedPsy-1.7B outperforms MedGemma-1.5-4B by 11.42 points despite being less than half its size. On HealthBench-Hard, MedPsy-4B surpasses MedGemma-27B by 15.33 points, while MedPsy-1.7B surpasses it by 11.66 points despite being ~16x smaller. Beyond accuracy, MedPsy reduces average response length by 1.7x (1.7B) and 3.2x (4B) versus its Qwen3 backbones, while Q4_K_M quantization preserves the aggregate score within 1 point of BF16 with ~69% lower disk footprint. In real-device inference, MedPsy-4B's shorter generations translate into 75% lower median end-to-end latency and estimated energy per answer than its Qwen3 backbone at matched quantization, while peak RAM remains below 5 GB across our tested laptop and smartphone configurations.

Ankana Banerjee, Mark van der Giezen, Ole Gunnar Aasprong, Emiel A. M. Janssen, Øyvind Steinsbø, Tore Grimstad

Multimodal AI fusion of histology and clinical data is an active research area in computational pathology, but fusion methods are rarely compared under identical conditions and across different clinical endpoints. We present a modular framework in which slide-level embeddings, obtained by training an attention-based aggregator over patch embeddings from a frozen foundation model, and per-visit clinical embeddings are fed into 15 interchangeable fusion methods with a shared output dimension and prediction head (except decision-level methods). MedPhysBench: A Benchmark for Evaluating Language Models on Medical Physics Calculations and Error Detection

Omar Nusrat

Medical physicists turn a prescription into a delivered dose and check one another's calculations, because one error in a beam calibration or a monitor-unit (MU) calculation can affect every patient treated with it. MedPhysBench has ten seeded generators of calculation items in radiation therapy, diagnostic imaging, nuclear medicine and radiation protection, up to clinical tasks such as a TG-51 calibration worksheet. Reference answers come from code implementing the governing protocols, and a grader converts units and applies tolerance bands. From the same inputs, separate checking items test error detection, showing a colleague's worksheet or only the colleague's result, half correct and half with one planted error. On a held-out test split of 1,200 items (half for two models), 12 models scored 33.3 to 100.0% on calculation. Of the nine answers graded incorrect for the four best (99.3-100.0%), five contained model errors and four followed NCRP Report 151 on points our items misstate or leave open. Below them, most models did worst on level-3 clinical tasks, and Claude Sonnet 5.5 omitted the inverse-square correction in 19 of 20 isocentric MU items. Five models detected at least 96.7% of mostly large planted errors with at most 4% false alarms; for four of the other seven, checking only the result raised false alarms significantly. In an exploratory targeted test with 20 public cases of an omitted inverse-square correction, the three best models tested caught it in every worksheet, and three weaker models accepted 16 to 18 of 20 planted worksheets, one of them while applying the correction in most of its own calculations. Claude Opus 5.5 accepted 6 of 20 wrong results, although it applied the correction in all 20 of its own. We report the effect of two problems in our shielding items, found after the runs, and release the code, per-item outcomes and targeted-test responses.

Molecular Prior Networks

Pawel Gomoluch, Anthony Bradley

Predicting affinity between small molecules and their protein targets remains one of the central problems in drug discovery. Due to the substantial cost of wet-lab experiments, machine learning models of drug-target affinity are usually trained on relatively small datasets, ranging from tens to hundreds of molecules. Given the inherent difficulty of generalization between different targets and chemical series, existing datasets of protein-ligand affinities are typically not used for new projects. At the same time, tabular foundation models, including Prior-data Fitted Networks, have recently shown how transformers can be used to solve machine learning problems in a single forward pass of the network, after being trained over a large corpus of, typically, synthetic datasets. Inspired by that line of work, we propose a novel affinity modelling approach, in which the labels of the query molecules are predicted from the labelled support molecules in a single forward pass of a transformer model. The model is trained on thousands of existing affinity datasets, enabling it to capture the general patterns of molecular activity across different targets, chemical series and dataset sizes. For the smallest support sets, our model matches the state of the art on the regression variant of the FS-Mol benchmark, while being faster than existing methods, thanks to the lack of task-specific training. A comparison against a reduced variant of our architecture, which amounts to kernel regression in a meta-learned feature space, shows that the predictive performance of the transformer model is only partially explained by simple molecular similarity.
