Overview of CHIP 2026 Shared Task 1: Patient-Specific Phenotype Concept Recognition in Full-Text Biomedical Literature

Xuanlyu Zhu, Feng Tian, Yang Zhong, Pengqi Zheng, Xiwen Bi, Ling Luo

Automatic phenotype information extraction plays an important role in rare disease diagnosis, genotype-phenotype analysis, and precision medicine. However, accurately identifying phenotype concepts and determining their associations with specific patients remain challenging, particularly in full-text biomedical articles involving multiple patients and complex clinical descriptions. This paper presents an overview of CHIP 2026 Shared Task 1, which aims to promote the development of methods for patient-specific phenotype concept recognition from full-text biomedical literature. The task consists of two subtasks: full-text phenotype concept recognition and patient-specific phenotype association. For this evaluation, we used 200 annotated full-text biomedical articles from the PatientPheX dataset, containing 506 patients, 15,193 phenotype mentions, and 5,656 patient-phenotype associations. A total of 455 teams registered for the competition, with 189 and 103 valid submissions received on the Phase A and Phase B leaderboards, respectively. The best-performing system achieved an overall score of 0.7389 on the final test set, outperforming the baseline by 8.37 percentage points. The top-performing teams explored diverse approaches combining biomedical language models, large language models (LLMs), and ensemble strategies. The evaluation results highlight both the potential of these approaches and the remaining challenges in patient attribution and phenotype normalization from full-text biomedical literature. The post-evaluation phase remains open at https://tianchi.aliyun.com/competition/entrance/532512.

Visualizing and Interpreting Temporal Care Team Dynamics for Cancer Survival Risk Analysis

Yuhua Huang, Hsiao-Ying Lu, Kwan-Liu Ma

Cancer care is a longitudinal, time-sensitive process requiring coordination among diverse healthcare professionals (HCPs), whose collaboration evolves throughout treatment. Prior studies link care team structure to patient outcomes, but the temporal dynamics of collaboration and their relationship to evolving patient risk remain poorly understood. We introduce a visual analytics framework for modeling and interpreting time-varying care team interactions in oncology. We construct dynamic collaboration networks from electronic health record (EHR)-mediated communication data and use a temporal graph neural network (TGNN) to estimate patient outcome risk over time. We then investigate how changes in care team composition and specialist participation relate to shifts in estimated risk across treatment stages. Our multi-view interactive system combines TGNN-based risk modeling with coordinated visualizations of care team networks, feature-level explanations from a surrogate logistic regression model, and exploratory what-if analysis. Without making causal claims, the system helps analysts examine how risk estimates co-evolve with collaboration patterns and identify care team characteristics associated with these changes. We demonstrate its utility through expert-guided analysis scenarios involving 505 patients with breast, colorectal, and lung cancers, evaluating its ability to reveal clinically meaningful collaboration patterns. This work improves the interpretability of temporal graph-based models in longitudinal, team-based healthcare, with potential applicability to other evolving collaborative settings.

A Visual Grounding Benchmark for Retinal OCT with Multimodal Large Language Models

Ronald Fecso, José Morano, Botond Fazekas, Paul Widmann-Sedlnitzky, Dorota Himel, Laetita Hinterhuber

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) can answer clinical questions about medical images, but trustworthy use requires visual grounding: models should localize the pathology they report rather than rely on spurious shortcuts. Whether MLLMs can visually ground clinical findings in retinal optical coherence tomography (OCT), the most widely used imaging modality in ophthalmology, remains largely unexplored. To address this gap, we introduce RetinaGroundBench, the first benchmark for biomarker visual grounding in retinal OCT. It comprises 815 B-scans exhaustively annotated by ophthalmologists with 4,537 bounding boxes covering 13 biomarkers across 8 diseases. Because MLLMs often fragment a single lesion into multiple partial predictions, we propose AggIoU, a metric that evaluates whether the union of overlapping predicted boxes covers the annotated region. Across 27 open-source MLLMs (0.8-38B parameters), we find that visual grounding remains largely unsolved: the best general-purpose model achieves a macro F1 of just 23.7%, with consistently low recall, despite reaching up to 73% balanced accuracy on biomarker presence questions. To narrow this gap, we developed RetGrounder, an MLLM fine-tuned using an OCT-specific strategy integrating grounding and textual information. Despite its relatively small size (8B), RetGrounder improves grounding F1 to 33.9%, a 43% relative improvement. Our benchmark provides a reference point for measuring progress in the field of OCT diagnosis with MLLMs.

The Value of System 1 Models in Medicine

Xiufeng Yang, Konstantinos Vrettos, Zeineb Ben Chaaben, Fabian Drexel, Jannik Lübberstedt, Johannes Moll

Many clinical decisions are a choice among a few fixed options, taken quickly and in large numbers. Such decisions also need an uncertainty estimate to know when to defer to a clinician. Generative language models answer slowly in free text and state their confidence poorly. In contrast, System 1 models answer zero-shot with a probability for each answer. However, their performance across clinical tasks is unknown. Four System 1 models, two proprietary and two with open weights, were evaluated zero-shot on 86 public tasks from 14 clinical domains in 20 languages, spanning examination questions and medical imaging. Tasks were assigned to six capability tiers before analysis. The primary outcome was the area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve (AUROC). Secondary outcomes included calibration, language effects and comparisons with published results and generative models. Across 179,111 cases, the mean AUROC of the best model per task was 0.94 for medical knowledge, 0.93 for text understanding, 0.83 for clinical judgement from text, 0.89 for image perception, 0.84 for 2D image findings and 0.69 for volumetric findings. Imaging tasks scored 0.09 (95% CI, 0.06 to 0.11) lower than text tasks (P < 0.001). The best model matched or exceeded the best published result on 13 of 26 text tasks and on 8 of 35 imaging tasks. Triage nurses outperformed the best model (accuracy, 0.85 vs. 0.56). One decision took 0.34 to 0.44 seconds and 24 to 81 joules on open-weight models run locally. Accuracy on identical questions fell by up to 39 percentage points from English to Yoruba. Generative models of the same family were 11 to 76 times slower, with 3 to 5 percentage points higher balanced accuracy. System 1 models decide quickly, cheaply and with usable confidence when the answer is stated in a text or recalled from medical knowledge, but not when it must be judged or read from an image.

From Structured Records to Patient Narratives: Preserving Clinical Alignment in LLM-Generated Health Summaries

Bayram Ayadi, Florian Geissler, Johanna Schmidhuber

As wearable and digital health systems increasingly generate longitudinal physiological records, communicating these observations in patient-accessible language remains a significant challenge. Large Language Models (LLMs) offer a promising mechanism for translating structured clinical information into readable narratives, but this process may introduce semantic drift, where clinically precise meaning is distorted during simplification. We investigate evidence-grounded health summarization for menstrual health monitoring using a constrained generation framework that combines structured evidence conditioning with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) using a prompt-based constrained generation framework. We evaluate semantic alignment using Natural Language Inference (NLI), atomic factuality metrics, and readability measures. Across three LLMs and 4,500 generated summaries, explicit grounding constraints consistently improve clinical alignment, particularly by improving uncertainty preservation and, once faithfulness is evaluated against retrieved evidence, generally preserving or improving faithfulness, while effects on omission remain model-dependent. We also find that improved clinical alignment comes at the cost of increased reading difficulty and medical terminology. These findings highlight the need to jointly evaluate faithfulness, uncertainty preservation, and readability when developing patient-facing medical LLM systems.

IndoNutriQA: An Indonesian Clinical Nutrition Question Answering Benchmark

Tirana Noor Fatyanosa, Ummu Ditya Erliana, Mahendra Data, Muhammad Nurwegiono, Eleazar Tadeo Eman, Alham Fikri Aji

Clinical Question Answering (QA) underpins Clinical Intelligent Decision Support Systems, but progress is severely limited for low-resource languages such as Indonesian by the absence of public domain-specific data. We introduce IndoNutriQA, an expert-curated Indonesian clinical QA benchmark of 911 question-answer pairs built from real clinical-nutrition reports. Rather than a large-scale training corpus, it is positioned as a high-quality evaluation resource: every pair is grounded in a clinically relevant context, enriched with structured metadata (anthropometric assessment, medical diagnosis, document provenance, chunk segmentation). Eight standardised question types following the Nutrition Care Process (NCP), including the Problem, Etiology, Sign and Symptom (PES) diagnosis statement, are answered for every report, which was written by a graduate-level dietetic intern, by one of three nutritionists. Answers are free-form clinical prose rather than extractive spans, and an expert validation study finds 6-10% of reference answers contestable. The dataset supports generative, extractive and retrieval-augmented QA as well as zero- and few-shot LLM evaluation. We report fine-tuned baselines (IndoBERT variants, mT5, RAG) with 5-fold cross-validation, learning curves and bootstrap 95% confidence intervals, and benchmark eight contemporary LLMs zero- and five-shot. We further calibrate our LLM-as-judge rubric against three expert raters (one RD, two nutritionists), finding it human-comparable on factuality and completeness but unreliable on clinical safety, and a human error analysis shows it misses unsupported content that the raters identify. IndoNutriQA is released to foster research in Indonesian clinical NLP and nutritional decision support.

GWDM: A Guided Wavelet Diffusion Model for Realistic Histopathology Image Generation

Abdullah, Tao Huang, Ickjai Lee, Lei Bi, Euijoon Ahn

Histopathology images are recognized as a reliable and comprehensive modality for Deep Learning (DL) based diagnosis. However, effectiveness is constrained by limited large-scale annotated datasets, as collecting expert-labeled medical data is costly and time-intensive. Recent advances in generative models, particularly diffusion models, demonstrate strong capabilities in synthesizing medically relevant content for dataset augmentation. Existing generative frameworks treat histopathology patches as generic natural images, failing to enforce biological constraints such as histomorphological structures and heterogeneous staining patterns essential for valid synthesis. We propose a guided wavelet diffusion model (GWDM) framework that captures image details at multiple scales using wavelets and leverages controllable conditioning based on domain-specific histopathological features (e.g., tissue patterns and color variations). We further formulate a histomorphological guidance loss applied to real and generated patches. A loss ramping strategy gradually introduces these constraints during training, ensuring stable optimization and robust modeling of tissue architecture. GWDM was evaluated on three benchmark datasets (BCSS, MoNuSeg, and PAIP2019), consistently outperforming state-of-the-art generative methods. Ablation studies confirmed that domain-guided conditioning, perceptual loss, and structural similarity loss each contribute meaningfully to synthesis quality. Downstream nuclei and tissue segmentation experiments further validated the clinical utility of GWDM-generated images, demonstrating consistent performance improvements when synthetic data augments real training sets. This work shows that domain-specific conditioning and dual-loss optimization generate realistic histopathology patches, reducing data scarcity and improving clinical downstream segmentation performance.

Automated screening of privacy risks in medical AI

Konstantinos Vrettos, Eleftherios Tzanis, Haridimos Kondylakis, Evangelia E. Vassalou, Florence X. Doo, Michail E. Klontzas

Medical artificial intelligence (AI) models are often shared without their training data, assuming this protects the patients who contributed them. However, a model can reveal whether a patient's records were used in training, a privacy breach known as a membership inference attack. This risk is usually reported as a cohort average, which can appear negligible while individual patients are almost certainly exposed. Identifying potentially vulnerable records before developing a production model could make privacy assessment more practical. We measured each patient's vulnerability to membership inference attack, across 7,789 models trained on six clinical datasets in four modalities, including two adapted pretrained medical models. Training choices changed how many patients had high attack scores, but the patients who ranked highest by risk largely stayed the same across the configurations we tested. Stronger weight decay reduced the exposed cohort up to threefold, yet largely the same patients remained most at risk across training recipes, architectures and initializations, including foundation models. Therefore, we developed a lightweight screening tool that uses four small models to identify patients with high risk of exposure before production-model training. We also assessed if flagged patients could be protected without removing their data. Privacy screening should become incorporated into data preparation for clinical AI.

Large language models show systematic failures in incorporating evolving medical knowledge

Min Zeng, Zaifu Zhan, Kai Yu, Rui Zhang

Medical knowledge evolves as clinical guidelines revise recommendations, yet whether large language models (LLMs) faithfully incorporate such changes remains unclear. We introduce ChronoEvo, comprising 1,709 recommendation-level revisions across 12 forms of medical knowledge change, to distinguish recognition of revised guidance from incorporation into clinical recommendations. In the primary model, 90.8% of responses recognized revisions and 80.2% were clinically correct, but only 58.5% fully incorporated guidance, yielding a 32.3-percentage-point recognition-incorporation gap. This dissociation persisted across 14 models and was corroborated by two clinicians' adjudicated assessment of 300 responses, yielding a 40.3-percentage-point gap in the validation sample. Changes in recommendation strength and patient eligibility were associated with retention of outdated guidance. A revision-salience intervention reduced outdated-content retention and improved clinical correctness but did not significantly improve full incorporation. These findings identify faithful incorporation of evolving medical knowledge as a distinct challenge for clinical LLM reliability, even when updated guideline evidence is available.

RetiJev: Fast and Source-Grounded Ophthalmic Decision Models

Bo Liu, Lianyu Wang, Meng Wang, Ching-Yu Cheng, Huazhu Fu

Ophthalmic clinical decision support requires fast, accurate, and privacy-preserving inference, yet autoregressive large language models (LLMs) incur substantial latency and computational overhead for decisions among predefined actions. We therefore investigate a decision-oriented alternative to generative reasoning. Specifically, we first introduce RetiJev-Data, a source-grounded ophthalmology corpus comprising 78,158 clinical-decision and diagnosis questions derived from articles in PubMed Central and a MeSH vocabulary covering 531 eye diseases, and 50,074 guideline-grading questions spanning 11 grading systems, along with a 24,662-question evaluation benchmark. Based on this, we develop RetiJev, a family of 0.8B-9B decision models that directly score candidate answers without autoregressive decoding. Across all model sizes, RetiJev outperforms both a 27B LLM and the commercial Jev 1.13 decision model on guideline grading by 10.4-23.0 points. RetiJev-9B further achieves the highest accuracy on clinical decisions, improving over both baselines by 5.4-6.5 points while matching them on diagnosis. It answers a case in 158 ms on one GPU, 3.5x faster than LLM generation and 470x faster than reasoning. Models are released at https://github.com/Awenbocc/RetiJev.

From Clinical Risk Prediction to Operational Decisions: An Uncertainty-Aware Framework for ICU Staffing

Jiayan Yang, Ad Feelders, Arno Siebes

Advances in electronic health records (EHR) and large-scale critical care databases have enabled the development of machine learning models for the early detection of patient deterioration. While these models often achieve strong predictive performance for outcomes such as sepsis and mortality, most studies evaluate them purely on discrimination metrics.
