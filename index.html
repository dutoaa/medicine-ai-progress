OmniTongue: A Comprehensive MLLM Benchmark for Automated Tongue Diagnosis in Traditional Chinese Medicine

Liuyi Xu, Yun Guo, Shusheng Li, Xuewen Zhang, Yining Qian, An-Yang Lu

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have excelled in Western medical imaging, yet their potential in Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM) tongue diagnosis remains systematically unevaluated. Tongue diagnosis requires sophisticated global and local perception, serving as a rigorous touchstone for assessing the visual understanding depth of MLLMs. However, existing datasets are limited by single dimensions, coarse granularity, and ambiguous labels, hindering the evaluation of authentic clinical capabilities. To bridge this gap, we introduce OmniTongue, the first comprehensive, fine-grained Visual Question Answering benchmark for TCM tongue diagnosis. This benchmark integrates 1,351 expert-validated images and 21,616 instruction pairs. Its core design features 16 clinical dimensions for multidimensional evaluation, region-specific annotations for pathological localization, and 66 fine-grained labels for precise diagnostic representation. Extensive evaluations of existing MLLMs reveal significant performance gaps, demonstrating that medical-specific models lack domain advantages, while universally poor Macro-F1 scores expose a reliance on statistical shortcuts rather than genuine understanding of tongue pathology. These findings not only highlight the current limitations of MLLMs in interpreting regional medical features but also underscore OmniTongue's significance for developing robust automated tongue diagnosis models. The code and dataset will be made publicly available in a future release.

TabBioMed: A Large-Scale Benchmark for Biomedical Tabular Learning

Pau Mateo-Bernadó, Saivenkata Nagavyjayanthi Polapragada, David Bonet, Pol Arbiol Rakuljic, Laia Mogas Pladevall, Marçal Comajoan Cara

Recent advances in machine learning have accelerated biomedical prediction, but evaluation on structured biomedical data remains fragmented, especially for datasets with high dimensionality, large sample size, missingness, and class imbalance. We introduce TabBioMed, a large-scale benchmark of 94 curated public biomedical tabular datasets spanning electronic health records, drug discovery, genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, metabolomics, single-cell omics, and systems biology. TabBioMed unifies these datasets through a standardized, dataset-aware preprocessing and evaluation framework, enabling reproducible comparison across classification, regression, and multi-target tasks. We benchmark 23 models across classical baselines, gradient-boosted trees, neural tabular architectures, and tabular foundation models. Foundation models deliver the strongest family-level predictive performance. However, their gains are regime-dependent: tree-based models and tuned multilayer perceptrons remain highly competitive, often dominating under inference-time or training-cost constraints. Overall, TabBioMed shows that biomedical tabular learning is strongly context-dependent, with optimal model choice depending on data regime, task type, and computational budget. We release TabBioMed with curated datasets and splits, preprocessing code, baselines, and results, providing a reproducible benchmark for practical model selection and future methods development in biomedical machine learning, available at https://github.com/AI-sandbox/TabBioMed.

Hard to Halt: Automation Bias in Agent-Driven Sequencing Prior Authorization Workflows

Nie, M.; Chung, W.; Waxler, J.; Lee, M.; Weng, C.; Lewis, R.; Ahimaz, P.; Wang, K.; Liu, C.

PurposePrior authorization (PA) for exome or genome sequencing (ES/GS) is a time-consuming process that impedes timely rare disease diagnosis. Large language model (LLM)-based browser agents offer potential for automating these workflows, but their clinical reliability remains uncharacterized. MethodsWe built a sandbox with a simulated ES/GS payer portal and a synthetic electronic health record (EHR) of 836 patient profiles, including compliant profiles and deficient profiles with designed issues. Gemini 3 Pro, Gemini 3 Flash, and Claude Opus 4.5 were evaluated on task completion, form accuracy, and appropriate withholding. Two replication experiments were run on 100 profiles to assess run-to-run variability of agent performance. ResultsGemini 3 Pro (95.5%) and Claude Opus 4.5 (93.7%) completed far more tasks than Gemini 3 Flash (56.1%), consistently across runs. Without agentic scaffolding, Gemini 3 Pro flagged designed issues in 91.3% of deficient profiles but also halted on all compliant profiles. ConclusionCurrent LLM-based browser agents show a systematic bias toward form submission. A modular, multi-agent architecture with human supervision may be warranted for safe clinical deployment.

Learning to Defer under Expert Drift

Ziyao Mou, Andrea Wynn, Eric Nalisnick

The learning to defer (L2D) framework enables safety in machine learning and AI systems by allocating difficult or critical decisions to a human expert. However, most prior work on L2D assumes fixed expert reliability, overlooking temporal fluctuations in human performance due to factors such as fatigue and cognitive biases. Humans commonly exhibit such substantial, systematic performance shifts across a wide range of domains, from high-stakes settings such as radiology, aviation, and judicial decision-making, to everyday tasks such as crowdsourced image annotation. We propose Expert-Drift-Adapted Learning-to-Defer (EDA-L2D), a novel expert-aware L2D framework that explicitly models these temporal shifts by conditioning the deferral head on recent histories of expert outcomes via a sequence model. This history-aware approach enables adaptive deferral policies that anticipate reliability fluctuations and better allocate decisions between human and machine over time. We provide comprehensive experiments across three diverse tasks -- image classification, medical diagnosis, and hate speech detection -- including generalization analyses under realistic deferral conditions and irregular shifts in expert reliability. Our results show that EDA-L2D adapts more effectively than prior L2D methods to diverse temporal patterns of expert reliability, resulting in improved system performance and more effective deferral decisions. Code is available at https://github.com/ZiyaoMou/L2D-Expert-Drift.

Auditing black-box clinical prediction models with a large language model through queries alone: a blinded test with planted flaws

Zhiyu Liu, Xinyu Yang

Large language models (LLMs) are used to explain clinical prediction models, on the promise that they can say what a model computes, find its faults and even reveal new knowledge in it. We tested the three promises where the answers could be checked. From the International Stroke Trial we trained four kinds of model (logistic regression, explainable boosting machine, gradient-boosted trees, neural network) for two outcomes on patients randomised earlier, planted one flaw in each of 24 copies (an under-predicted subgroup, a reversed risk factor or an implausible step) and mixed them with the 8 clean models. GPT-6 Astra audited each model three times, blind, through a tool that only returned the model's predictions; the judging rules were committed before any run and applied by program. Its surrogate models, with a median of 128 terms, reproduced the audited models' log-odds with a mean $R^2$ of 0.976 (0.959 for an additive surrogate of 42 terms fitted by an analyst). It found the planted flaw in 69 of 72 runs (96%; 95% CI 80% to 99%); a script written for the purpose found all 24. It also reported many more problems: 60% of 1,089 checkable reports failed the pre-specified check against the model's average behaviour; post hoc, most of these were real but small steps, and 15% of the checkable reports were not found in the model. Of 601 unexpected relationships it read out of the clean models, 11% held in patients randomised later (random pairs 6%; a regression scan's shorter list of its strongest candidates, 28%); after a check on the training data, 33% against 30%. With query access alone, an LLM can describe a black-box model faithfully and find nearly every planted flaw, as a purpose-written script also did; its reports of problems must be checked on the model and its discoveries on data.

A Neurosymbolic Multimodal Framework for 30-Day Diabetic Readmission Prediction: Integrating Structured EHR Data, Dense Clinical Embeddings, and Knowledge-Grounded Symbolic Extraction

We propose a neurosymbolic multimodal framework evaluated on the public Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset (101,766 encounters; 71,518 patients) with a patient-level grouped 80/20 split. Three streams are fused: engineered structured features; 32 components of Bio_ClinicalBERT note embeddings obtained by truncated singular value decomposition; and symbolic entities extracted by an open-weight clinical language model within a three-stage hybrid retrieval-augmented generation pipeline (dense retrieval, sparse lexical retrieval, and cross-encoder re-ranking) with reflective negation auditing. On the held-out test cohort (n = 20,153), the structured baseline achieved an area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) of 0.6477, sensitivity of 0.5839, and Brier score of 0.1080. The full multimodal model achieved the highest sensitivity (0.6667; +8.28 percentage points), the best F2-score (0.3824), and the lowest Brier score (0.0977), at a slightly lower AUROC (0.6375) and specificity (0.5161). Shapley additive explanations showed that prior utilization dominates risk, with compressed narrative components among the top predictors. On a 500-note code-switched benchmark, grounded retrieval raised Hinglish symptom recall from 4.8% to 40.2% and precision from 3.1% to 45.2%, while reducing entity hallucinations from 1.74 to 0.00 per note.

Adversarial Robustness of Capsule Networks for Medical Image Classification

Srinivasan, A.; Sritharan, D. V.; Chadha, S.; Fu, D.; Hossain, J. O.; Breuer, G. A.; Aneja, S.

PurposeDeep learning models are increasingly being used in medical diagnostics, but their vulnerability to adversarial perturbations raises concerns about their reliability in clinical applications. Capsule networks (CapsNets) are a promising architecture for medical imaging tasks, given their ability to model spatial relationships and train with smaller amounts of data. Although previous studies have focused on adversarial training approaches to improve robustness, exploring alternative architectures is an underexplored direction for combating poor adversarial stability. Prior work has suggested that CapsNets may exhibit improved robustness to adversarial perturbations compared to convolutional neural networks (CNNs), but performance on adversarial images has not been studied systematically in clinical environments. We evaluated the robustness of CapsNets compared to CNNs and vision transformers (ViTs) across multiple medical image classification tasks. MethodsWe trained two CNNs (ResNet-18 and ResNet-50), one ViT (MedViT), and two CapsNets (DR-CapsNet and BP-CapsNet) on four distinct medical imaging datasets (PneumoniaMNIST, BreastMNIST, NoduleMNIST3D, and BloodMNIST) and one natural image dataset (MNIST). Models were evaluated on adversarial examples generated by projected gradient descent, fast gradient sign method, AutoAttack, Carlini & Wagner, and black-box transfer attacks. Models also underwent PGD adversarial fine-tuning to assess whether architectural differences persisted after adversarial training. Interpretability experiments, including latent space and Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) analyses, were conducted to better understand model stability on adversarial inputs. ResultsUnder conventional training, CapsNets demonstrated superior robustness under adversarial perturbations compared to CNNs and ViTs across all medical imaging datasets and the natural image dataset. Latent space and Grad-CAM visualizations revealed that CapsNets maintained more consistent feature representations and attention maps after adversarial perturbations compared to CNNs and ViTs, suggesting that advantages in CapsNet robustness are supported, at least in part, by more stable feature encodings. PGD adversarial fine-tuning improved robustness across models, with MedViT exhibiting the largest gains and thereby narrowing the performance gap. ConclusionCapsNets exhibit advantages in adversarial robustness over CNN- and ViT-based models on medical imaging tasks. These findings suggest that capsule-based architectures may be a promising direction for robust medical image classification. Future work should validate CapsNet robustness across more complex medical imaging tasks, independent external datasets, and broader sources of real-world imaging variability.
