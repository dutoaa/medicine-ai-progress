Redefining Synovial Pathotypes in Psoriatic Arthritis through Multi-Modal Histology and Machine Learning (The PSYNOVIUM Study)

Hum, R. M.; Christofi, M.; Smith, S. L.; Tan, Y.; Shukla, R.; Marshall, L. M.; Smith, M.; Williams, A.; Orozco, G.; Viatte, S.; Buch, M.; Barton, A.; Ho, P.; Martin, P.

BackgroundSynovial inflammation in psoriatic arthritis (PsA) lacks a validated histological classification framework. Current approaches apply rheumatoid arthritis (RA) synovial pathotypes to PsA, which overlooks immunobiological differences between the diseases and limits the predictive power of tissue stratification. We aimed to define data-driven, PsA-specific synovial pathotypes to facilitate tissue-driven precision medicine. MethodsIn this cross-sectional study, ultrasound-guided synovial biopsies were obtained from 61 patients with PsA (n=22), seropositive RA (n=23), seronegative RA (n=10), and other inflammatory arthritides (n=6). Sections underwent multi-modal staining, including H&E, picrosirius red, and immunohisto-chemistry for CD3, CD20, CD68, CD138, and CD31. Whole-slide digital image analysis and automated pixel classifiers quantified cellular and stromal metrics. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering identified intrinsic PsA pathotypes, which were correlated with clinical disease activity, radiographic osteoarthritis (OA), and longitudinal treatment responses. FindingsWhile a subset of PsA patients shared an inflammatory signature with seropositive RA, unsupervised clustering of the PsA cohort revealed three distinct, novel pathotypes: Dense Myeloid (macrophage-driven), Fibro-Stromal (fibrosis and pauci-immune), and Angio-Lymphoid (hypervascular with significant CD138+ plasma cell infiltration). Pathotypes were independent of treatment and concurrent radiographic knee OA. The Fibro-Stromal pathotype correlated with significantly higher cutaneous psoriasis severity. Longitudinally, patients with the Fibro-Stromal pathotype exhibited a trend toward lower treatment response (44%) following standard interventions (methotrexate or TNF inhibitors) compared to the Dense Myeloid (50%) and Angio-Lymphoid (80%) pathotypes. InterpretationWe present the first computationally derived synovial pathotypes specific to PsA, revealing distinct plasma cell and fibrotic architectures not captured by RA-centric models. To enable clinical translation, we distilled these findings into a pragmatic, four-marker diagnostic decision tree (CD31, CD68, CD138, and picrosirius red) designed for routine histopathology workflows, providing a staging ground for biomarker-stratified clinical trials in PsA. FundingFunding from the NIHR Biomedical Research Centre: Manchester, Arthritis UK, the Wellcome Trust, Brit-PACT, the National Psoriasis Foundation, and the Medical Research Council. Research in contextO_ST_ABSEvidence before this studyC_ST_ABSThe potential clinical utility of tissue-based patient stratification is well established in rheumatoid arthritis (RA), where baseline synovial pathotypes (lympho-myeloid, diffuse-myeloid, and pauciimmune) have guided targeted biologic therapy in landmark precision medicine trials. However, psoriatic arthritis (PsA) exhibits a fundamentally distinct synovial microenvironment, characterised by unique vascular architecture, altered stromal fibroblast phenotypes, and distinct immune infiltrates intertwined with the IL-23/IL-17 cytokine axis. Consequently, attempting to classify PsA synovial tissue using established RA-derived scoring systems risks misrepresenting intrinsic disease biology. There is currently no validated, disease-specific histological classification system for PsA, which forces therapeutic decision-making to rely on an empirical, trial-and-error approach rather than underlying target tissue pathobiology. Added value of this studyTo our knowledge, this is the first study to define data-driven, computationally derived synovial pathotypes entirely specific to PsA. By leveraging multi-modal digital pathology and unsupervised machine learning, we deconstructed the PsA synovium into three biologically distinct pathotypes: Dense Myeloid, Fibro-Stromal, and Angio-Lymphoid. We demonstrated that the highly inflammatory PsA microenvironment is distinctly driven by CD138+ plasma cells rather than the CD20+ B cells typically observed in RA. Furthermore, we identified a striking tissue-level divergence: patients with the highly fibrotic, pauci-immune Fibro-Stromal pathotype exhibited the most severe cutaneous psoriasis and demonstrated a trend toward higher failure rates on standard therapies, independent of concurrent osteoarthritis. Crucially, we translated these high-dimensional computational findings into a pragmatic, four-marker diagnostic decision tree (incorporating CD31, CD68, CD138, and picrosirius red). Implications of all the available evidenceThis study establishes that PsA synovial pathology requires a dedicated, disease-specific classification framework. The proposed diagnostic decision tree can be integrated into routine clinical histopathology workflows without the immediate need for advanced spatial or single-cell transcriptomics. Furthermore, the correlation between the Fibro-Stromal pathotype, severe skin disease, and poor joint response to standard therapies provides a biological rationale for stratifying this specific subgroup towards early IL-17 or IL-23 inhibitor intervention alongside conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs. Ultimately, these novel PsA pathotypes provide the vital staging ground required to design and execute biomarker-stratified, tissue-driven clinical trials, paving the way for precision medicine in PsA.

Applications of Fairness Training in Artificial Intelligence to Ophthalmic Disease Diagnosis using External Eye Photographs

Kostin, V.; Yoon, S.-H.; Chen, J. S.; Baek, S.; Seid, J.; Quyang, N.; Li, E.; Li, E.; Shi, M.; Wang, M.

Diabetic retinopathy (DR), age-related macular degeneration (AMD), and glaucoma are leading causes of preventable blindness, yet whether external eye photographs carry phenotypic information about posterior segment disease remains unclear, and methods to mitigate demographic disparities in such models are largely unexplored. We developed and evaluated deep learning and vision-language models for binary classification of vision-threatening DR (VTDR), advanced AMD, and glaucoma using 40,687 external eye photographs from 9,878 patients at Massachusetts Eye and Ear. Convolutional networks (WResNet, EfficientNet), a vision transformer (ViT), and a finetuned vision-language model (LLaVA) were assessed by area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), with fairness across sex, race, and ethnicity quantified by the equity-scaled AUC (ES-AUC). Five fairness-training strategies were applied to the best models. LLaVA achieved the highest baseline AUC for VTDR (85{middle dot}4%, comparable to ViT 82{middle dot}6%); advanced AMD was similar across architectures (EfficientNet 86{middle dot}3%; LLaVA 84{middle dot}8%; ViT 84{middle dot}7%), whereas glaucoma was substantially worse (67-74%). Fairness training improved VTDR and AMD AUCs to 86{middle dot}3% and 85{middle dot}9% while partially mitigating demographic disparities. In summary, although AI models that rely on external eye photography to detect posterior segment disease may not yet be suitable for clinical deployment, the fairness methods demonstrated in this proof-of-concept study could be applied to other AI systems to decrease performance disparities and potentially improve model performance.

Agentic Pipeline for Large-Scale Clinical Data Queries in Enterprise Electronic Health Records

Pawar, K.; Tavabi, N.; Kalepalli, A.; Kalepalli, N.; Blackmon, M.; Williamson, A.; Kiapour, A.

Using electronic health record (EHR) data to answer clinical research questions is challenging. It requires understanding the research question, finding relevant information across different sources, and combining that information to produce meaningful results. We developed an agentic large language model (LLM) pipeline that decomposes investigator-authored requests into source-specific tasks, generates and executes SQL on EHR, integrates structured and unstructured EHR evidence, and applies study-specific clinical note adjudication. We evaluated this workflow against data analyst-identified cohorts across 50 pediatric orthopedic clinical data query requests. Of these, 43 were included in manual-review-adjusted performance analyses. The median adjusted positive predictive value, sensitivity among patients identified by either approach, and corresponding F1 score were 0.93 (95% CI, 0.88-0.98), 0.92 (95% CI, 0.90-0.95) and 0.90 (95% CI, 0.87-0.94), respectively. These findings support the feasibility of translating natural-language research intent into executable, autonomous multi-stage EHR workflows.

BackgroundProper adoption of AI in emergency medicine necessitate thorough and rigorous appraisal. Classic benchmarks, which rely heavily on basic question-and-answer formats, though dominating the benchmarking field of LLM usage in medicine, have proven inadequate in assessing the full spectrum of skills incumbent upon an agent in healthcare. MethodsWe propose an intricate benchmarking scheme in which the skill set required of an emergency physician is tested and quantified. Our benchmarking structure is 3-fold: skills such as history taking, diagnostic accuracy, treatment plan, and empathy are evaluated by an emergency medicine expert, while the same expertise is scored by an intern from the perspective of a patient, through what we call a simulated emergency room visit. A comprehensive written examination is also utilized to measure general emergency medicine knowledge. ResultsEight LLMs were benchmarked against each other and in contrast to nine emergency medical doctors. Furthermore, the models demonstrated superior performance in comprehensibility, credibility, and empathy. When comparing AI models against each other, performance advantages were marginal during simulated emergency room visits and were mostly pronounced during the written examination. ConclusionAI models demonstrate a clear advantage over human physicians across a diverse range of emergency medicine competencies. The proposed benchmarking scheme provides a comprehensive metric for a thorough evaluation of medical skill set, furthering validation, adaptation, and utilization of AI in emergency medicine.

Exploring Data Augmentation for Stroke Severity Prediction: How Generative AI Enhances EEG Signal Classification

Sarkar, M. S. S.; Saon, M. T. H.; Basak, S.

In clinical datasets, data augmentation is often avoided because it can remove important unusual values, known as outliers, which are important for understanding diseases accurately. However, clinical EEG datasets often suffer from two key limitations: small sample sizes and class imbalance, which significantly reduce classification performance. To over-come these challenges, this study applies generative AI-based data augmentation techniques with a carefully designed hybrid pipeline that effectively enhances performance even on limited EEG data. Using EEG signals from 50 brain stroke patients [1], we investigate both traditional oversampling methods (SMOTE and ADASYN [2]) and advanced generative models, including WGAN and WGAN-GP. Our proposed pipeline involves pre-processing EEG recordings, extracting comprehensive statistical and frequency-domain features, applying feature reduction, and evaluating multiple machine learning classifiers. Experimental results show that the combination of SelectKBest feature selection, SVM classifier, and WGAN-GP augmentation achieves the highest classification accuracy of 72%, outperforming other methods. This work introduces a novel hybrid approach for EEG-based stroke severity classification, demonstrating that generative augmentation, when integrated properly, can significantly improve performance on small clinical datasets. To the best of our knowledge, this combination of techniques has not been explored before on any dataset, providing a solid foundation for future research in automated EEG-based stroke diagnosis.

Serum brain-derived tau distinguishes acute neurotrauma diagnoses

Shi, R.; Svirsky, S. E.; Balogun, W. G.; Zeng, X.; Fedak, J. D.; Nafash, M.; Brodman, S. T.; Okonkwo, D. O.; Puccio, A. M.; Karikari, T. K.

Traumatic brain injury (TBI) and spinal cord injury (SCI) are two common forms of neurotrauma that can present with overlapping clinical features, complicating early diagnosis and management particularly in acute and mixed-trauma settings.1-3 Accurate and rapid biological differentiation remains an unmet need.4 We hypothesized that brain-derived tau (BD-tau), an ultrasensitive blood-based biomarker preferentially selective for tau protein originating from the brain, would distinguish TBI from SCI. In a multi-cohort assessment of 342 patients admitted to the neuro-intensive care unit (285 with TBI, 57 with SCI), and 22 controls, baseline serum BD-tau concentrations were markedly elevated in TBI compared with SCI, discriminating between the two populations with high accuracy (area under the curve [AUC]=0.91, 95%CI 0.87-0.96). In paired serum-cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) samples, serum BD-tau significantly outperformed CSF BD-tau for injury-type discrimination (AUC=0.95, 95%CI 0.89-0.99 vs. 0.80, 95%CI 0.71-0.89). Baseline serum BD-tau also showed superior discrimination of TBI from SCI compared with established blood biomarkers of neurotrauma, including neurofilament light chain, glial fibrillary acidic protein, and ubiquitin C-terminal hydrolase L1 (AUC=0.82, 95%CI 0.76-0.88; vs. 0.53, 95%CI 0.43-0.63; 0.67, 95%CI 0.58-0.76; 0.71, 95%CI 0.64-0.79; all DeLong p<0.001). Longitudinally, serum BD-tau remained persistently elevated in TBI, but not SCI, for up to 144 hours post-injury, with distinct temporal trajectories between injury types. Together, these findings indicate that serum BD-tau enables rapid and minimally invasive differentiation of TBI from SCI and may support early clinical decision-making, triage, and enrollment into injury-specific therapeutic trials, highlighting the potential of circulating biomarkers to improve precision diagnostics in acute neurotrauma care.

Multi-Agent Generative Artificial Intelligence for Transfusion Reaction Feature Extraction

Kumari, A.; Jacobs, J. W.; Dilly, L.; Anand, A.; Woo, J.; Lin, Y.; Shih, A. W.; Booth, G. S.; Raslan, I.; Relke, N.; Dubey, R.; Srivastava, S.; Abdulwahab, W.; Goel, R.; Raza, S.

We sought to develop and evaluate an open-source, multi-agent artificial intelligence (AI) pipeline to extract transfusion reaction features defined by the National Healthcare Safety Network (NHSN) Hemovigilance Module from clinical notes. METHODSWe curated 181 features from the NHSN protocol and developed a multi-stage pipeline that extracted features, their timing, and clinical details from free-text notes. Three open-weight large language models (Qwen 3.6-27B, GPT-OSS-120B, and Kimi K3) were applied to 50 notes from expert-written scenarios, published case reports, and MIMIC-IV discharge summaries. RESULTSPhysicians agreed on 1,201 of 1,343 co-rated feature occurrences (89.4%; Krippendorffs 0.73). Precision was above 0.98 for all models. Omissions accounted for more than 94% of false negatives. Timing and clinical details were judged supported or reasonable in more than 92% and 97% of assessments, respectively. Results were consistent across note sources and in analyses including discordant assessments. CONCLUSIONMulti-agent AI pipelines provide a feasible method for extracting transfusion reaction signals from clinical notes with high precision. Our work may have applications in active hemovigilance, reaction classification, and big data research.
