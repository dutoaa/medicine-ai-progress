Medical Specialty Prediction from Bangla Patient Narratives: A Comparative Study of ML, DL, Transformers, and LLMs

Tasnin Tabassum Ali, Faisal Ahmed, Muhammad Anwarul Azim

Timely access to appropriate medical specialists remains a bottleneck in Bangladesh's healthcare system, as patients describe symptoms in informal Bangla rather than clinical terms, leading to ineffective referrals. This paper compares four model families for predicting medical specialties from Bangla patient narratives: six machine learning (ML) models using hybrid TF-IDF features, four deep learning (DL) models using Word2Vec embeddings trained on the corpus, four fine-tuned transformers, and five large language models (LLMs) evaluated zero-shot. From a corpus of 3798 patient records covering 20 specialties, we retained the five with the most records (Medicine, Gynecology, Dermatology, Pediatrics, Gastroenterology) and split the resulting 2730 records 80:20 into training and test sets shared by all families. The best transformer was XLM-RoBERTa (86.26% accuracy, 86.21% macro-F1), the best ML model was SVM (84.98%, 84.92%), the best DL model was Bidirectional GRU (80.77%, 80.83%), and the best LLM was Gemini (90.29%, 90.15%). Holm-corrected paired McNemar tests show SVM significantly outperforming Bi-LSTM (adjusted p = 0.004), whereas the Gemini-versus-Grok gap does not survive correction (adjusted p = 0.104), and neither the strongest transformer versus the weakest LLM (Grok) nor SVM versus the strongest transformer reaches significance. A tuned classical model is thus statistically indistinguishable from the strongest transformer, so accuracy rankings alone should not be read as evidence of one family's superiority. The findings provide a comparative baseline for Bangla medical text classification.

Laya-IsoToken: Day-One Clinical Codebook Switching via Closed-Form Operator Compilation

Zeqiang Wang

Healthcare systems periodically revise diagnostic and procedural classifications (ICD-9 to ICD-10/11), update hospital reimbursement groupers (MS-DRG), and deploy clinical coding across languages. Supervised coding networks bind clinical text encoders to fixed per-code classifier weights, forcing hospitals to re-annotate tens of thousands of records before switching codebooks, whereas standalone large language models incur high latency and hallucinate invalid codes. Here we show that day-one clinical codebook switching becomes feasible without target-labeled notes when local clinical evidence reading is decoupled from a strictly frozen multilingual concept space. Laya-IsoToken trains a clinical span reader once on source records with zero per-code classifier parameters, separating text evidence from historical disease prevalence. Given only public target code descriptions, closed-form matrix compilation aligns distributional shifts, compensates for paraphrased definitions, and deduplicates sibling codes in under three seconds, while a pre-deployment geometric residual routes unfamiliar concepts to offline language model verification. Across 32,804 MIMIC-IV ICD-9 discharges, Laya-IsoToken matches published supervised baselines (62.6% Micro-F1) while reducing active calibration error by 32%. Switched with zero target training notes to 13,783 unseen-patient ICD-10 discharges, MS-DRG groups, and multilingual cohorts, it outperforms biomedical dense retrievers, semantic crosswalks (+5.4 F1 points on ICD-10, paired $d_z = 0.49$, $p < 0.001$), and official US CMS equivalence mappings (+3.5 points, $p < 0.001$) at 9.4 ms per admission. Pairing this reader with an offline 31B verifier across 1,000 full discharge summaries lifts zero-training ICD-10 coding to 40.6% Micro-F1 (57.0% category F1, $d_z = 0.66$, $p < 0.001$) with 0.0% invalid code hallucinations.

Can LLM Judges Catch Unsafe Clinical Actions? A Fault-Injection Study

Neda Ghafouri

As clinical AI agents begin to interact with electronic health records (EHRs), large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to evaluate their actions. But how reliably can these judges recognize clinically dangerous mistakes, especially when an agent appears to have completed its task successfully? We investigate this question using MedAgentBench by introducing controlled clinical errors into verified agent trajectories. The experiments include 416 faulty trajectories, eight error categories, and six open-weight LLM judges. By comparing each faulty trajectory with its correct counterpart, we measure whether judges genuinely recognize errors rather than simply flagging more actions as unsafe. The findings show that access to clinical evidence significantly improves judge reliability. When shown only the final answer, judges cannot distinguish errors that leave it unchanged, while flagging 56% of correct trajectories. With full trajectory information, the ability to distinguish unsafe actions from correct ones reaches a score of 0.75, and providing actual EHR responses improves discrimination by 0.39. Surprisingly, including the agent's reasoning does not improve overall performance and reduces detection of incorrectly reported clinical values by 0.18, which remain the hardest errors to detect. MedGemma performed worse than the general-purpose model and was the only judge to miss potentially dangerous medication overdoses. We also find that the benchmark's original grader accepts all 38 trajectories with an intentionally skipped laboratory check, while combining LLM judges with a rule-based checker informed by the full EHR record detects 97% of injected faults.

ASCENT: A Benchmark for Evaluating and Advancing Stepwise Diagnostic Reasoning in Large Language Models on Common Clinical Scenarios

Yera Choi, Yeong Hwa Kim, Jaedeok Lee, Taekang Kim, Sangdoo Yun, Seong-Eun Moon

Large language models (LLMs) excel at medical question answering yet are rarely evaluated on the stepwise diagnostic reasoning that defines real clinical workflows, where impressions are revised as information accumulates. We build Annotated Stepwise Clinical rEasoning for NaturalisTic Diagnosis (ASCENT), a clinician-annotated benchmark and training resource of 3,078 stepwise problems derived from MedQA-USMLE that decomposes each vignette into EMR-aligned steps (Findings, Impression, supporting Rationale), enabling evaluation of intermediate reasoning under incomplete information. Experiments and training with ASCENT revealed insights into how current LLMs handle stepwise diagnostic reasoning. Even strong reasoning models that perform well on MedQA-USMLE leave substantial headroom on ASCENT, and general-purpose frontier models trail further, exposing a persistent gap between fully informed and stepwise diagnosis. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-7B and 32B on ASCENT yields measurable F1 gains over both pre-trained and HuatuoGPT-o1 CoT-trained baselines, with gains driven primarily by precision. Complementary robustness analyses (counterfactual perturbation, format-vs-content control, judge agreement, and rollout) further show that ASCENT-fine-tuned models rely on the diagnostic content of prior impressions rather than imitating their output format, while error propagation under rollout remains a key challenge for clinical deployment.

A dataset of clinically similar image pairs from citation-linked case reports for medical image retrieval

Guy Shmueli, Keno K. Bressem, Tom Hope

Retrieving images of patients clinically similar to a given patient can support diagnosis, teaching and research. Training and evaluating retrieval systems requires knowing which images of different patients show the same finding, and existing datasets are small or narrow in scope. We construct PMC-PaIR, a dataset of 86,144 pairs of clinically similar images from 56,013 open-access PubMed Central case reports, which often document rare or challenging presentations. We develop an approach that follows citations, which often link case reports of similar patients. To determine what two linked cases share clinically, it prompts large language models to generate and answer hierarchical questions, from modality to diagnosis and findings, and to match images by caption using the answers. Experts find the pairs highly accurate, and this question-guided approach finds twice as many correct pairs as direct matching. Fine-tuning BiomedCLIP on PMC-PaIR improves retrieval in all modality, anatomy and diagnosis categories tested, for image-only queries and image queries with a short text naming the finding. We release the dataset and the automated pipeline, which can extend PMC-PaIR as new case reports are published.

Learning Multimodal Representations from Partially Paired, Small-Scale Data

Merel Kuijs, Till Richter, Rushin H. Gindra, Korbinian Traeuble, Christian Matek, Rebeka Luknárová

Modeling multimodal data from partially paired samples is critical for advancing domains like biomedicine, where vast unimodal datasets and foundation models exist but paired data remains scarce. Existing fusion methods rely on large-scale paired datasets, limiting their use in scenarios with incomplete pairing. We introduce CAMEO, an adversarial learning-based modality fusion framework that integrates modalities from small, partially paired datasets. Combining any pre-trained unimodal encoder with a cross-modal latent alignment mechanism, CAMEO learns shared representations requiring only minimal paired samples. Evaluated on computational pathology tasks such as niche classification and cell type composition prediction, CAMEO achieves superior data efficiency, outperforming contrastive approaches like CLIP in low paired-data regimes, and highlighting the benefits of adversarial alignment when paired annotations are scarce. To facilitate further research, we additionally release a fully annotated HuggingFace dataset comprising three organs and paired image and gene expression modalities. By extending fusion methods to address limited pairing and small-scale datasets, we provide a framework that advances multimodal learning and broadens its applicability to real-world biomedical problems.

JECT: Addressing the Context Shortcut in Joint-Embedding Predictive Learning for Chest CT

Hanbin Ko, Hong-Jun Choi, Jihun Kim, Young-Gon Kim, Dongheon Lee, Chang Min Park

Joint-embedding predictive architectures learn by predicting features of masked regions from visible context. In chest computed tomography, the findings that decide a diagnosis, such as a small nodule, are often not predictable from the surrounding anatomy. A hidden finding is then best predicted toward normal anatomy, and the prediction loss stops rewarding the encoder for representing it. We call this the context shortcut. Conditioning the predictor on the radiology report repairs the prediction, but in our setup the encoder gains little over image-only pretraining: the report supplies what the encoder never had to learn. JECT therefore gives the report two roles: a condition for the predictor and, through masked section-text prediction, a target for the encoder, which must predict the report's anatomical sections from the unmasked scan. A hierarchical three-dimensional encoder that predicts at every stage keeps this clinical content at every scale. In ablations, the text target improves classification and localization, while conditioning adds little to the encoder. Trained from scratch on 173k volumes, the recipe reaches the level of current computed tomography foundation models in classification, retrieval and nodule malignancy, and of task-specific models in lung-cancer risk prediction after fine-tuning. Conditioning still matters: its predictor can be reused, with no extra training, to show where a report sentence acts, changing most at the finding the sentence describes. Code and pretrained weights are available.

A multimodal foundation model for tissue proteomics and morphology in cancer

Benedikt von Querfurth, Lukas Klein, Cédric Vincent-Cuaz, Eeshaan Jain, Yexiang Cheng, Johann Wenckstern

Cancer diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment decisions increasingly rely on integrating tissue morphology with specific molecular readouts of the tumor and its microenvironment. H&E histopathology provides scalable morphology, whereas multiplex spatial proteomics quantifies dozens of proteins in situ with spatial context, but remains selectively deployed, often with different marker panels across cohorts. This creates a translational bottleneck: most patients lack high-depth molecular profiling, and existing models rarely integrate morphology and spatial molecular data in a single framework. We present VirTues-M2, a multimodal foundation model that jointly learns from H&E and spatial proteomics using a novel vision transformer-based architecture. By encoding marker identity during pretraining, VirTues-M2 is robust to missing modalities, imperfect modality pairing or registration, and variable marker panels, enabling unified inference for tissue phenotyping, panoptic cell segmentation, virtual staining, annotation-free biomarker discovery, and reinforcement learning-based adaptive marker panel selection to reduce assay burden. As a concrete demonstration of translational utility, in a newly collected cohort of 1,053 treatment-naive non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC) patients with paired spatial proteomics and H&E, VirTues-M2 discovers high-risk and low-risk tissue signatures that stratify overall survival in both the discovery cohort and an independent validation cohort, outperforming current state-of-the-art stratification schemes; all enabled by VirTues-M2's ability to jointly analyze both modalities within a single model.

Clinical3D-Bench: Evaluating and Improving Spatial Intelligence of Multimodal LLMs in Hospital Environments

Yang Bai, Yingzhi Xia, Ling Lin, Jun Cheng, Lin Fu, Huazhu Fu

Spatial reasoning in hospitals requires tracking look-alike equipment, interpreting patient-relative directions, and judging passage widths, yet existing benchmarks provide limited coverage of these abilities. We introduce Clinical3D-Bench, a simulated benchmark with 3,368 questions of eight types on egocentric walkthroughs of 373 hospital-room layouts. Layouts follow hospital design guidelines, answers are computed from the 3D scenes, and paired scene states test whether models change their answers when relevant evidence changes. Videos are photorealistic, and ten physicians screened the layouts. Among 35 multimodal large language models (MLLMs), the best open and proprietary models reach 45.7% and 53.6% macro accuracy, against 40.8% for an answer prior and 79.0% for physicians on a subset, and 27 of 32 open models score below 40% on two-way patient-relative directions. Fine-tuning on Clinical3D-45K, our 44,750-question training set, lifts two MLLMs 16-20 points above the best proprietary model. 3D maps given to the fine-tuned MLLM as input go largely unused: replacing them with another video's, even when they are ground truth, barely changes its accuracy. The proposed GeoJeV instead lets the MLLM compute with 3D: it reconstructs a scene memory from each video, selects 3D operations and their arguments from the MLLM's question state, and adds their evidence to its option scores. It achieves the highest accuracy of all evaluated models, narrowing the gap to physicians.

SciFig2Data: A Workflow for Recovering Structured Data from Scientific Figures

Xinhe Wu, Yicheng Ye, Yadong Jin

Scientific measurements reported in scholarly articles are often embedded in compound figures rather than available as machine-readable data. Recovering these measurements at literature scale requires an integrated workflow for figure localization, panel decomposition, chart identification, numerical reconstruction, and experimental context association. We present \textit{SciFig2Data}, an automated, high-throughput workflow for harvesting structured experimental data from scientific PDF repositories. The system integrates multi-scale document element detection, adaptive compound figure decomposition, two-stage OCR-based semantic filtering, Swin-based chart classification, and instruction-guided multimodal large language model (MLLM) reconstruction. It further incorporates directory-level batch execution, caching, and structured logging to support large-scale processing. We demonstrate the workflow on two biomedical literature corpora comprising 605 toxicology and pharmacology articles and 551 lipid nanoparticle (LNP) articles. The pipeline successfully reconstructed 468 and 242 tabular outputs from the respective corpora, illustrating its practical applicability to literature-scale scientific data harvesting. By associating reconstructed measurements with caption-derived experimental metadata, SciFig2Data provides a workflow for transforming graphical evidence in scholarly publications into structured, context-enriched data records for downstream AI4Science applications.

Leakage-Free Is Not Confounder-Free: What a Patient-Level Split Cannot Certify About a Medical Imaging Benchmark

Ignazio Gallo, Mattia Gatti, Leonardo Sgroi, Darwin Castillo, Yuliana Jiménez-Gaona, Nicola Landro

Patient-level splitting is the standard safeguard against subject leakage in medical imaging evaluation, but it cannot remove a confounder that every fold inherits: an acquisition variable aligned with the diagnosis across the whole cohort. We present a five-question audit of a candidate confounder: how well it predicts the diagnosis, whether it can be decoded from the images, whether diagnostic scores depend on it, whether the image adds predictive value beyond it and how much discrimination survives once it is controlled. We apply it to DMR-IR, the public dataset on which most machine-learning results in breast thermography rest. On 236 patients under a strict patient-disjoint protocol, the acquisition date alone predicts malignancy at AUROC 0.980, above every imaging arm we evaluated (best 0.949), because benign and malignant patients were recruited in different years. The acquisition year is decodable from the pixels among patients who share a diagnosis, largely through a global thermal offset outside the breast, yet only one arm, the one pooling every frame of a visit, demonstrably carries it into its diagnostic score. No arm improves on the date on paired folds, and once the date is controlled, discrimination either collapses or survives only on comparisons the benchmark cannot attribute. The dataset contains only eight cross-label same-date pairs, from ten patients, which limits reliable evaluation within recorded acquisition-date groups. A second, independently acquired cohort, whose dominant channel is patient age, reproduces every finding except legibility, from a confounder that is clinically legitimate. Mitigation may suppress such a confounder, but without cross-label acquisition support no evaluation on the benchmark can certify that what remains is pathology.

MedUIGym: Evaluating and Training GUI Agents for Clinical Workflows

Yitong Qiao, Lei Liu, Yue Shen, Jian Wang, Jinjie Gu, Zhixuan Chu

Automating clinical workflows requires vision-language agents that can navigate dense graphical user interfaces (GUIs) in electronic health records (EHRs) and execute precise actions, yet many medical agents still rely on text or structured tool calls. In this work, we introduce MedUIGym, a scalable interactive environment comprising 15,739 tasks from 17 benchmark sources across six clinical task families for training and evaluating clinical GUI agents. In MedUIGym, agents perceive EHR states from screenshots and execute mouse-and-keyboard actions, as well as a paired native-tool interface over identical clinical data. The environment verifies both final answers and required clinical actions, and supports parallel trajectory collection for training. Benchmarking twelve vision-language models shows that current agents struggle across diverse clinical workflows, and simply increasing reasoning effort fails to yield consistent gains. Furthermore, MedUIGym supports offline learning from GUI trajectories and online reinforcement learning from task rewards. After training, both Qwen3.5 models perform better on in-domain clinical tasks and generalize to tasks from external sources. By unifying tasks from existing benchmarks, dual-interface evaluation, and GUI trajectory collection, MedUIGym provides a testbed for studying how vision-language agents complete clinical tasks through the EHR's graphical interface. Our project page is available at https://github.com/yitong-qiao/MedUIGym.

Virtual spatial transcriptomics links kidney histopathology to disease-associated molecular programs

Nonchev, K.; Jang, Y.; Nlandu Khodo, S.; Koelzer, V. H.; Raetsch, G.; Moor, M. B.

IntroductionSpatial transcriptomics can resolve compartment-specific molecular programs in kidney disease, but its cost and technical requirements limit its use in clinical diagnostics and in large biopsy archives. Predicting spatial gene expression from routine histology with artificial intelligence could extend molecular characterization to existing clinically annotated kidney tissue collections. MethodsWe adapted DeepSpot-M, a pretrained multimodal foundation model that predicts spatial gene expression from histology, using paired hematoxylin and eosin images and Visium spatial transcriptomics from 209 Kidney Precision Medicine Project (KPMP) participants. We evaluated virtual profiles in held-out human biopsies, associated them with histopathologic and biochemical injury markers in 567 rats with defined nephrotoxin exposures from a public toxicogenomics database, and generated a virtual atlas from 1 534 histology slides of 476 additional KPMP participants. ResultsKidney-specific adaptation of DeepSpot-M doubled the mean Pearson correlation of each models 1 000 best-predicted genes (0.43 versus 0.22) and exceeded recent foundation models. Virtual profiles preserved the direction of all 13 disease-marker differences in measured expression, including tubular injury, maladaptive repair, gluconeogenesis, and fibrosis-associated programs. All 11 measured associations between molecular programs and estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) were also preserved. Without rat-specific training, a virtual injury score distinguished moderate or severe histopathologic lesions (area under the curve, 0.85) and at least twofold blood urea nitrogen elevation (0.82). In our atlas of 5.46 million virtual spots, higher proximal tubule program scores were associated with higher eGFR (partial r, 0.32) and less interstitial fibrosis (-0.57), and program-pathology associations agreed with those of measured Visium profiles in independent participants (Spearman{rho} , 0.64). ConclusionsKidney-adapted virtual spatial transcriptomics links histology-derived molecular programs to experimental renal injury and human clinical and histologic phenotypes. Our approach enables spatial molecular analyses in routine biopsy histology, which could help decipher disease-associated pathways in existing biopsy archives. Translational StatementSpatial molecular profiling of kidney biopsies is informative but too costly for routine use, whereas hematoxylin and eosin sections exist for nearly every biopsy. We show that a model adapted to paired kidney histology and spatial transcriptomics infers programs of tubular injury, metabolic differentiation, and fibrosis from routine histology, and that these programs track kidney function and pathology across 476 participants. Applied to existing archives, virtual spatial transcriptomics could stratify heterogeneous patients by tissue molecular state, prioritize biopsies for targeted assays, and support biomarker discovery and trial enrichment once validated prospectively.
