TransXplainRRG: A Radiologist-Evaluated LVLM for Chest Radiograph Report Generation

Chayan Mondal, Duc-Son Pham, Tele Tan, Ashu Gupta, Vincent T. Y. Tiong, Shreya Ghosh

Recent advancements in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLM) enhance X-ray report generation, but often neglect clinical context, overlook workflow integration, and lack explainability, which hinders clinical adoption due to interpretability and reliability concerns. This paper addresses the dual challenges of performance and expert explainability in automated X-ray report generation for clinical AI applications. Our proposed approach, TransXplainRRG, utilises an off-the-shelf Swin Transformer model along with a transformer-based text encoder that incorporates patient medical history to generate a radiology report. Further, we explore an expert-guided `inside-out' approach and extract only abnormal findings for radiology report refinement. Thus, this study bridges the gap between high-performance automation and interpretability critical for clinical practice by combining state-of-the-art transformer-based vision encoders, text encoders, and LLMs. TransXplainRRG is trained and tested on the large-scale MIMIC-CXR dataset and further evaluated on the unseen IU X-Ray dataset to demonstrate its generalizability and robustness. We employed the GREEN metric as a complementary measure of clinical consistency, achieving scores of 0.327 on MIMIC-CXR and 0.605 on IU X-Ray, both higher than those achieved by MAIRA-2 under the same evaluation setting. It also performed competitively across standard evaluation metrics, frequently matching or surpassing state-of-the-art models. Additionally, we introduce qualitative evaluation metrics developed from radiologists' viewpoints and conduct a preliminary radiologist evaluation on 100 generated reports to assess their clinical relevance. Quantitative and qualitative analyses further demonstrate the potential of our TransXplainRRG framework for reliable and explainable radiology report generation.

Curiosity-0826-4B: Generative Medical Event Sequence Modeling Across Pediatric and Adult Populations

Mani Nagarathnam, Luke Benham, Paul Blazek, John Cashy, Kevin Chung, John Muenning

We introduce Curiosity-0826-4B, an updated version of the generative medical event foundation model trained on Epic Cosmos that simulates patient timelines to produce zero-shot clinical predictions. Curiosity-0826-4B incorporates a broader patient population and richer clinical event representations, while generally matching or exceeding Curiosity-0226-1B, the largest model in the previous Curiosity-0226 series, on evaluated tasks supported by both models. Laboratory value prediction improved, with MAE 18--46% lower than that of Curiosity-0226-1B. We evaluated prediction of disease events across pediatric cohorts aged 2--17 years. AUROCs for new asthma diagnosis (0.742--0.785) and asthma exacerbation (0.829--0.852) were comparable to those in the adult reference cohort, while AUROCs for sickle cell crisis (0.820--0.847) were lower than in adults (0.915). Explicit representation of documented death enabled new mortality prediction tasks among adults across hospice, inpatient, and ambulatory settings (AUROC 0.821--0.942). Scaling-law analysis guided compute-optimal initial pretraining followed by continued pretraining with context extension. The resulting Curiosity-0826-4B model has more parameters (4.3B versus 1B) and a longer context window (16K versus 8K). It processes 56% fewer training tokens and requires 2.2 times the training floating point operations (FLOPs) compared with its predecessor. By extending a common zero-shot prediction strategy to pediatric patients and additional outcomes, Curiosity-0826-4B broadens opportunities to develop clinical and operational applications without training separate task-specific models. Further validation should assess local calibration, performance across patient subgroups and event frequencies, timeline completion rates, and generalization to new settings, alongside prospective evaluation of clinical and operational utility.

Evaluating Pretrained Language Semantics for Structured EHR Prediction Tasks

Ashvin Gupta, Brendan Delaney, Alessandra Russo

Structured Electronic Health Records (EHR) models typically treat each clinical concept as a dataset-specific token, discarding the natural-language semantics associated with the event. As a result, relationships between concepts must largely be learned from EHR co-occurrence, without directly exploiting any prior semantic knowledge. We investigate how pretrained causal language models can be adapted for longitudinal EHR prediction by developing tEHR. Here, we represent events with their natural language description and use this text stream to apply EHR-adaptive continued pretraining, followed by task-specific discriminative fine-tuning using LoRA, utilising the LLM as a longitudinal patient-encoder. We evaluate this approach across four prediction tasks on the MIMIC-IV dataset and pancreatic cancer prediction on the CPRD dataset at 1 to 12 month horizons, with tEHR using the Mistral-7B-v0.3 backbone achieving the strongest overall performance. Ablations show that performance declines when clinical descriptions are removed or misaligned, when numerical values are omitted, and when either stage of adaptation is removed, while explicit elapsed-time descriptions provide little additional benefit. Analysis of the learned patient representations further reveals clinically coherent structure associated with distinct patterns of disease risk and clinical history.

Fine-Tuned Small Language Model for Synthetic Clinical Data Generation and Predictive Healthcare Analytics

Mihnea Sebastian Burlacu, Ionut Anghel, Tudor Cioara

The reduced availability of medical datasets represents an important challenge in the development of predictive models for remote healthcare solutions, especially when the available data are imbalanced or difficult to extend through traditional methods. Small Language Models (SLMs) have become a promising alternative to Large Language Models (LLMs) for addressing these limitations due to their lower computational requirements and adaptability to specialized tasks. However, their performance remains highly dependent on the quality and representativeness of the available training data. In this paper, we propose a method for clinical data augmentation based on a fine-tuned Microsoft Phi-2 model that is used to generate synthetic instances, with the purpose of improving deep learning-based model for predicting remotely monitored vital signs. The Phi-2 model was adapted to the medical domain through supervised fine-tuning on the MedQuad dataset, through a Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation (QLoRA)-based method and by applying few-shot prompting techniques. The resulting model was used afterwards in the augmentation of a small-sized dataset, composed of vital sign values and labels associated with pathological and physiological states. The experimental results demonstrate that fine-tuned SLMs improved the performance on medical domain, achieving higher scores for both lexical (ROUGE) and semantic (BERTScore) evaluation metrics compared to the pretrained model. Moreover, the fine-tuned SLM is effective for generating structured synthetic clinical data. When used for data augmentation, the generated dataset improved the performance of a LSTM predictive model, increasing the classification accuracy from 89.0% to 93.7% and showing consistent improvements in precision, recall, and F1-score.

A frontier machine intelligence for human neuroimaging

Chenhui Zhao, Akshay Rao, Akhil Kondepudi, Yiwei Lyu, Xinhai Hou, Rikhil Seshadri

Medical imaging is essential for diagnosing disease, guiding treatment and monitoring patients over time. Although artificial intelligence has advanced image interpretation, existing medical models often address restricted tasks, while general-purpose proprietary models lack access to private clinical imaging required to achieve medical expertise. Developing models that can interpret complex examinations and communicate their findings in the context of clinical decisions remains a major challenge. Here, we show that Neuro-VL, a vision-language model trained on nearly one million neuroimaging studies from routine clinical care, can interpret complete imaging examinations and engage in natural language conversations about their findings. Across internal and international datasets, Neuro-VL outperformed frontier general-purpose and specialized medical models in diagnosing, localizing, and describing abnormalities, as well as generating comprehensive radiology reports. The model also compared serial examinations to assess disease progression and more reliably resisted misleading clinical assumptions than leading frontier models from OpenAI and Google. These results extend health system learning beyond individual predictions to interactive image interpretation and longitudinal disease monitoring. They demonstrate how the collective clinical experience of health systems can be used to develop medical AI with specialized expertise and broad clinical capabilities.

Capacity-Constrained Delayed Feedback for Online Conformal Risk Control

Bowen Wang, Osvaldo Simeone

Online conformal risk control (OCRC) enables reliable uncertainty quantification in machine learning by converting model outputs into prediction sets with long-term risk guarantees. These guarantees, however, hinge on the assumption that the loss of each round is revealed immediately and losslessly. In practice, feedback may be delayed, and limited resources may prevent tracking all pending predictions, causing some feedback to be lost. To address this challenge, we develop capacity-aware OCRC (CA-OCRC), a framework that jointly determines how to allocate limited tracking feedback capacity and how to construct prediction sets. CA-OCRC is based on a randomized tracking policy that samples a proxy delay to decide whether, and for how long, feedback for the current round is tracked. CA-OCRC updates the prediction sets via inverse probability weighting, compensating for the possibility of feedback lost due to delays and limited tracking capacity. We analyze risk bounds for both arbitrary and stochastic delays, revealing a trade-off between receiving more feedback and avoiding capacity saturation. Building on these bounds, we optimize the proxy delay distribution, showing scaling laws for the tracking capacity that ensure the same long-term risk as OCRC with unlimited feedback. Experiments on medical imaging, natural language processing, computer vision, and wireless communications show the effectiveness of CA-OCRC as compared to existing OCRC mechanisms.

Redefining Synovial Pathotypes in Psoriatic Arthritis through Multi-Modal Histology and Machine Learning (The PSYNOVIUM Study)

Hum, R. M.; Christofi, M.; Smith, S. L.; Tan, Y.; Shukla, R.; Marshall, L. M.; Smith, M.; Williams, A.; Orozco, G.; Viatte, S.; Buch, M.; Barton, A.; Ho, P.; Martin, P.

BackgroundSynovial inflammation in psoriatic arthritis (PsA) lacks a validated histological classification framework. Current approaches apply rheumatoid arthritis (RA) synovial pathotypes to PsA, which overlooks immunobiological differences between the diseases and limits the predictive power of tissue stratification. We aimed to define data-driven, PsA-specific synovial pathotypes to facilitate tissue-driven precision medicine. MethodsIn this cross-sectional study, ultrasound-guided synovial biopsies were obtained from 61 patients with PsA (n=22), seropositive RA (n=23), seronegative RA (n=10), and other inflammatory arthritides (n=6). Sections underwent multi-modal staining, including H&E, picrosirius red, and immunohisto-chemistry for CD3, CD20, CD68, CD138, and CD31. Whole-slide digital image analysis and automated pixel classifiers quantified cellular and stromal metrics. Unsupervised hierarchical clustering identified intrinsic PsA pathotypes, which were correlated with clinical disease activity, radiographic osteoarthritis (OA), and longitudinal treatment responses. FindingsWhile a subset of PsA patients shared an inflammatory signature with seropositive RA, unsupervised clustering of the PsA cohort revealed three distinct, novel pathotypes: Dense Myeloid (macrophage-driven), Fibro-Stromal (fibrosis and pauci-immune), and Angio-Lymphoid (hypervascular with significant CD138+ plasma cell infiltration). Pathotypes were independent of treatment and concurrent radiographic knee OA. The Fibro-Stromal pathotype correlated with significantly higher cutaneous psoriasis severity. Longitudinally, patients with the Fibro-Stromal pathotype exhibited a trend toward lower treatment response (44%) following standard interventions (methotrexate or TNF inhibitors) compared to the Dense Myeloid (50%) and Angio-Lymphoid (80%) pathotypes. InterpretationWe present the first computationally derived synovial pathotypes specific to PsA, revealing distinct plasma cell and fibrotic architectures not captured by RA-centric models. To enable clinical translation, we distilled these findings into a pragmatic, four-marker diagnostic decision tree (CD31, CD68, CD138, and picrosirius red) designed for routine histopathology workflows, providing a staging ground for biomarker-stratified clinical trials in PsA. FundingFunding from the NIHR Biomedical Research Centre: Manchester, Arthritis UK, the Wellcome Trust, Brit-PACT, the National Psoriasis Foundation, and the Medical Research Council. Research in contextO_ST_ABSEvidence before this studyC_ST_ABSThe potential clinical utility of tissue-based patient stratification is well established in rheumatoid arthritis (RA), where baseline synovial pathotypes (lympho-myeloid, diffuse-myeloid, and pauciimmune) have guided targeted biologic therapy in landmark precision medicine trials. However, psoriatic arthritis (PsA) exhibits a fundamentally distinct synovial microenvironment, characterised by unique vascular architecture, altered stromal fibroblast phenotypes, and distinct immune infiltrates intertwined with the IL-23/IL-17 cytokine axis. Consequently, attempting to classify PsA synovial tissue using established RA-derived scoring systems risks misrepresenting intrinsic disease biology. There is currently no validated, disease-specific histological classification system for PsA, which forces therapeutic decision-making to rely on an empirical, trial-and-error approach rather than underlying target tissue pathobiology. Added value of this studyTo our knowledge, this is the first study to define data-driven, computationally derived synovial pathotypes entirely specific to PsA. By leveraging multi-modal digital pathology and unsupervised machine learning, we deconstructed the PsA synovium into three biologically distinct pathotypes: Dense Myeloid, Fibro-Stromal, and Angio-Lymphoid. We demonstrated that the highly inflammatory PsA microenvironment is distinctly driven by CD138+ plasma cells rather than the CD20+ B cells typically observed in RA. Furthermore, we identified a striking tissue-level divergence: patients with the highly fibrotic, pauci-immune Fibro-Stromal pathotype exhibited the most severe cutaneous psoriasis and demonstrated a trend toward higher failure rates on standard therapies, independent of concurrent osteoarthritis. The proposed diagnostic decision tree can be integrated into routine clinical histopathology workflows without the immediate need for advanced spatial or single-cell transcriptomics. Furthermore, the correlation between the Fibro-Stromal pathotype, severe skin disease, and poor joint response to standard therapies provides a biological rationale for stratifying this specific subgroup towards early IL-17 or IL-23 inhibitor intervention alongside conventional synthetic disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs. Ultimately, these novel PsA pathotypes provide the vital staging ground required to design and execute biomarker-stratified, tissue-driven clinical trials, paving the way for precision medicine in PsA.

KQML-Liver: Interpretable Hybrid Quantum Machine Learning with Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks for Liver Disease Classification

Nhat-Long Nguyen, Viet-Loi Dinh, Trong-Nghia Pham

Liver disease remains a major global health concern, highlighting the need for reliable and accessible early detection methods. Although recent advances in machine learning, deep learning and quantum machine learning (QML) have introduced promising diagnostic tools, many approaches suffer from limited interpretability and inflated performance caused by data leakage or overly simplistic evaluation protocols. These constraints reduce their reliability in real-world clinical practice. This paper proposes KQML-Liver, a hybrid framework that integrates QML with Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs) to enhance predictive performance and interpretability. The architecture combines parameterized quantum circuits for expressive feature representation with KAN-based functional mappings that capture nonlinear relationships in an interpretable manner. Evaluated on the Indian Liver Patient Dataset (ILPD) under a rigorous preprocessing protocol preventing data leakage, the proposed model achieves 82% classification accuracy and 100% recall, detecting all positive cases, outperforming existing baselines while providing improved transparency in clinical predictions. These results suggest that hybrid KAN-quantum architectures offer a promising direction for interpretable AI-assisted medical diagnosis.

Constructing and Validating Memorisation Audits for EHR Foundation Models

Inigo Stibbe, Joshua Placidi, Padmanabhan Ramnarayan, A. Aldo Faisal

Autoregressive electronic health record (EHR) foundation models may disclose training information at inference, yet clinical predictability makes patient-specific memorisation difficult to distinguish from generalisation. We evaluate fixed-window token reconstruction, sensitive-attribute disclosure, and random patient-level canaries in GPT-2-style models trained on MIMIC-IV, across early, best-validation, and final checkpoints. Reconstruction improves with additional context, but training--held-out differences remain small. Trajectory-unique prompts containing limited clinical information produce no excess sensitive disclosure for training patients. In a matched model with random binary patient-level canaries, training-patient label-recovery AUROC increases from 0.512 to 0.549 while held-out recovery remains at chance. An overfit 1,000-patient positive control achieves near-perfect recovery, confirming sensitivity to strong memorisation. These results reveal weak cohort-level memorisation but no reliable patient-level disclosure under the evaluated attacks. They motivate clinically grounded inference-time audits that can detect patient-specific memorisation in existing models without retraining with canaries.

Hx2Dx: From Anamnesis to Diagnosis - A Multimodal Arena for Conversational Clinical Reasoning in LLMs

Federico Ravenda, Mirko Cesarini, Daniele Montagnani, Volodymyr Karpenko, Antonietta Mira, Andrea Raballo

Large language models (LLMs) achieve expert-level diagnostic accuracy in static evaluations, where the model receives a curated vignette or case report that already condenses the diagnostically relevant findings. Such evaluations measure inference over already selected evidence; the evaluations do not account for the inquiry used to obtain it. In clinical practice, however, diagnosis is an iterative process of inquiry. Clinicians take the patient's history, or anamnesis (Hx), through targeted questioning, examine the patient, and request and interpret tests, progressively narrowing the differential until they reach a final diagnosis (Dx). We introduce Hx2Dx ARENA, an interactive benchmark of 1'000 clinical cases from the New England Journal of Medicine (NEJM). Each case is a simulated encounter that proceeds from initial presentation through history taking and physical examination to diagnostic workup. We evaluate open- and closed-source LLMs under multiple configurations. Performance varies considerably across models in the interactive setting, and every model loses diagnostic accuracy relative to the static setting, where the full case is presented upfront. The ability to ask the right questions accounts for much of both the gap between models and the loss of accuracy: diagnosis improves as questions become more targeted and build a more complete clinical picture. LLMs thus reason well once the relevant data are available but remain limited in hypothesis-driven information gathering, in knowing what to ask in order to know. Closing this gap is a prerequisite for their safe integration into real-world diagnostic workflows.

Do Large Language Models Show Racial and Sex Bias in Pain Management? A Counterfactual Audit of GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama, DeepSeek and Qwen

Richard J. Young, Alice M. Matthews

Racial and ethnic minority patients receive less pain treatment than non-Hispanic White patients, and large language models (LLMs) used as medical AI could reproduce that bias. Prior audits tested a few proprietary models, stated race explicitly and rarely measured the models' own response instability. In exploratory, non-preregistered analyses, Study 1 had four open-weight LLMs assess six acute-pain vignettes in every combination of four races and two sexes, with identity stated or cued only by a name (two names per group), comparing each identity with a White male reference on the same case and sampling seed. In Study 2, 24 configurations of 17 models, including OpenAI's GPT-4.1 mini and gpt-oss, Anthropic's Claude Haiku 4.5, Google's Gemini 2.5 Flash, Meta's Llama, DeepSeek and Qwen, with reasoning on and off and at two precisions, answered the same vignettes and the 50 clinician-written Q-Pain cases. In this single-prompt, JSON-constrained task, no difference survived correction in Study 1, none of 1,386 comparisons survived the false discovery rate in Study 2, and every pain-severity difference was equivalent to zero within one point on a 0-10 scale (within a quarter point for 237 of 238 Q-Pain comparisons). Opioid decisions were bounded less precisely (Study 1 could not exclude differences of about 20 percentage points), so they show no detected difference, not proof of none. The few differences surviving correction within a model favoured Black patients. Models differed far more from one another and across their own settings than between patients (the reference patient received the high opioid dose in 6% to 93% of responses), although serving software, provider and prompt format also differed. Fairness audits of clinical AI should use several names per group, pair comparisons on the seed, report the noise floor and test each deployed configuration.

Parameter-Efficient Conciseness Adaptation: Differentiable Length Control for Concise Clinical Reasoning in Smaller Language Models

Tanzeel Abbas, Ahmad Kamran Malik, Nafees Qamar, Sana Liaqat

Smaller language models (SLMs) make it feasible to deploy clinical natural language processing where large proprietary models are too costly, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning allows them to be specialised on commodity hardware. One obstacle persists after training: on medical tasks these models answer at far greater length than the clinical question requires. In clinical decision support and point-of-care use, where time is short, such verbosity is hard to act on even when the underlying reasoning is sound. Existing length-control methods depend on preference optimisation, reward models, or proprietary teachers that undercut the accessibility making SLMs attractive. We introduce Parameter-Efficient Conciseness Adaptation (PECA), which adds a single differentiable conciseness term to the QLoRA supervised fine-tuning objective. Because the token count is discrete and non-differentiable, PECA instead raises the end-of-sequence probability once generation exceeds a token budget, a smooth signal optimised during ordinary training, with no preference data, no auxiliary model, and a single commodity GPU. On a 1.7-billion-parameter model fine-tuned on the medical-o1-reasoning-SFT corpus, PECA reduces mean response length by 72.8% while improving multiple-choice diagnostic accuracy from 40.8% to 44.0%. A sweep over the conciseness strength traces an interpretable accuracy-to-length frontier for balancing brevity and diagnostic completeness.
