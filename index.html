TrialDesignBench: Benchmarking Language Models for Phase-I Trial Design Against Clinical Knowledge

Yihan Hank Tang, Peng Yang, Jiayi Xin, Tianqi Shang, Xuyang Chen, Ying Yuan

AI is used in much of the clinical trial pipeline. However, selecting the statistical design of a trial, a crucial step that determines how drug doses are tested on humans and evaluated, has seen almost no AI progress. LLMs appear to be a natural fit given their strengths in biomedical knowledge and statistical reasoning. Nevertheless, there is currently no appropriate way to evaluate such abilities because recent regulatory and methodological shifts, most notably FDA's Project Optimus, have redefined what constitutes a reasonable design and rendered many previous designs obsolete. To lay the groundwork for AI-assisted trial design, we introduce TrialDesignBench, a benchmark of 617 Phase-I/I-II oncology dose-finding trials, each paired with an expert-validated reference design that is consistent with the latest FDA regulatory initiatives. This reference standard is produced through a formalization of the expert design process. We evaluate five frontier LLMs, none of which matches the reference standard. Our ablation experiments trace this performance gap to two main causes, inference of latent, unrecorded design determinants from trial sources (supplying them raises class-wise balanced accuracy by 0.22) and structured decision-tree reasoning (+0.24). TrialDesignBench offers a reproducible, contemporary-standard objective for evaluating and enhancing AI-assisted trial design.

Operation-aware Multimodal Clinical Decision Support System for ICU Discharge Risk Assessment

Guoqing Zhang, Rafik Hadfi, Takayuki Ito

Unplanned 30-day intensive care unit (ICU) readmission is a critical benchmark for evaluating healthcare quality. Most predictive models assume readmission risk depends solely on a patient's individual pathophysiological state, ignoring hospital operational context and resource capacity. In practice, discharge decisions are dynamic trade-offs between patient stability and bed turnover pressures. To address this gap, we develop an Operation-aware Multimodal Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) for ICU discharge risk assessment. The system combines multi-agent collaborative extraction with self-evolving retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to eliminate template noise from progress notes, while constructing departmental workload indicators alongside admission flow features, continuous vital signs, and sparse medical codes (ICD/DRG). Addressing severe class imbalance (about 10.5% readmissions) and alarm fatigue, we implement data normalization, class reweighting, and decision-threshold calibration. In 20 independent runs across 20,000 ICU stays from MIMIC-III, empirical findings demonstrate that: vital signs alone have limited predictive capacity under real-world class imbalance (PR-AUC of 0.1387 +/- 0.0086); directly concatenating raw text embeddings degrades decision tree performance (XGBoost and LightGBM PR-AUC drop to 0.3893 and 0.4030); whereas an end-to-end multimodal fine-tuning architecture (Transformer E2E FineTune) utilizing layer-wise parameter freezing and differential learning rates reverses text negative transfer, achieving the highest AUROC of 0.8288 +/- 0.0110, PR-AUC of 0.4373 +/- 0.0310, and F1-Score of 0.4757 +/- 0.0196. Operational indicators supply independent risk signals, laying an engineering foundation for high-utility clinical decision support tools.

Differentiable Inverse Rendering Recovers Intrinsic Properties from Intravascular Optical Coherence Tomography

Ajay Manicka, Karim Kadry, Sebastian Ruiz-Lopera, Maryam Alsharqi, Sanjana Arji, Samuel Ruiperez-Campillo

Medical images encode intrinsic tissue properties that indicate disease progression, yet these properties remain entangled with the system-dependent physics of image formation. Differentiable inverse rendering asks whether they are recoverable by inverting the imaging system's forward physics rather than through post-hoc correction. We address this in optical coherence tomography (OCT), focusing on the optical attenuation coefficient, a per-pixel marker of pathology and cellular composition. Conventional recovery relies on closed-form inversions limited to simplified light-tissue models, or learned methods coupled to specific imaging configurations. We instead recover per-pixel attenuation from a single OCT image by gradient descent through a differentiable physics-based forward model. We verified the method against analytical solutions, validated it on five in vitro phantoms, and applied it without modification to 29{,}269 clinical intravascular OCT images from 128 patients. The inversion needs no training data or segmentations and is portable across platforms and retrospective archives. Replacing the forward model or recovering another optical parameter leaves the optimization procedure unchanged. We demonstrate per-pixel recovery is tractable because gradient information scales to this high-dimensional problem, where gradient-free methods become intractable. Our work supports quantitative tissue characterization from OCT images and provides a framework for extension across imaging modalities and organ systems.

Weight-Space Programming for Medical Imaging: Do We Still Need Training Data?

Maximilian Lindholz, Richard Ruppel, Annette Milnik, Sebastian Halskov, Semil Eminovic, Charlie A. Hamm

Can a large language model (LLM) write the parameters of a medical image classifier from medical knowledge alone, without any training data? Such classifiers normally require labelled images, which are costly and often unavailable. We investigate this question through weight-space programming (WSP), in which an LLM writes or adapts the weights and biases of a neural network directly, without optimisation on training data. Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra each generate the parameters of a small LeNet-style CNN in a single reply, without tool use and without access to any image. We compare these networks against zero-shot MedSigLIP and against the same architecture trained on labels using the Adam optimiser, across 7 classification tasks in four modalities: chest X-ray, breast ultrasound and abdominal CT (all from MedMNIST), and pelvic MRI (SynthUterus). The LLM-written networks perform above chance on all six MedMNIST tasks, with a mean test AUC of 0.719 for Opus and 0.711 for Astra. Both models outperform MedSigLIP on 5 of 6 tasks while using more than 10,000 times fewer parameters. However, the same network trained with Adam on only 256 labelled images achieves a higher AUC on every MedMNIST task (mean AUC 0.828). On SynthUterus, Opus attains an AUC of 0.764, whereas Astra falls below chance (AUC 0.136) owing to a systematic error. These results show that LLMs can write functional medical classifiers, but that they do not yet replace training data. We release the protocol, logs and all generated weights at https://github.com/MaximilianLindholz/weight-space-programming-medical.

AI in the Clinical Documentation Loop: Effects of LLM-Induced Note Bloat and Diagnostic Overcall on Downstream LLMs

Jinghui Liu, Anthony Nguyen, Taylan Gurgenci

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to process medical records for healthcare tasks such as clinical documentation. Because these tools also generate documentation, the relationship is now bidirectional: models both read from and write to the record. Understanding how LLMs behave when reading records that already contain LLM-authored content is therefore critical for their safe deployment. In this study, we examine how LLM-authored content in the medical record affects downstream LLMs that read the same record. We consider two types of LLM-induced artifacts: bloat, in which LLM-generated summaries accumulate in the record and change the length and provenance of its content over time; and distortion, in which LLMs subtly alter clinical findings rather than fabricating facts outright. As a case of distortion, we study diagnostic overcall (documenting an unconfirmed differential diagnosis in the record) and test whether it propagates to the reader LLM's output. Across seven reader models from four families, we find evidence of both effects. First, LLM-generated summary notes modestly but consistently degraded downstream hospital-course generation compared with copy-forward and templated text of matched length. Second, injected overcalls reappeared in downstream outputs at rates that varied by reader model, without detectable changes in standard quality metrics. These findings show the need to monitor AI-generated content in medical records and to account for it when those records are later processed by AI.

Discussion: The LLM was the main detector and NER added place names; asked to verify its own findings, the LLM discarded a quarter of true PHI. Conclusion: A local LLM layered with deterministic detectors offers recall-first and precision-first operating points without sending text off-site.

The Dark Matter of Clinical Data: An Identification Limit on Disentangling Physiology from Care Process in electronic health records

Mohammad Shahin, Krittaphas Chaisutyakorn, Dimitrios Proios, David Restrepo, Vishala Mishra, Boya Zhang

Representations learned from electronic health record (EHR) data encode patient physiology together with the clinical process that decides what is measured, when, and for whom. We formalize the data-generating process with latent physiology, ordering intent, laboratory values that exist only when measured, a learned representation, and clinical environments, and analyze two strategies for undoing this entanglement. First, no division of the information carried by laboratory "value" and "ordering" channels is identified from a single dataset: when ordering depends on the analyte, its distribution among untested patients is not recoverable, and a sentinel code for the undefined recorded value makes it carry all the entropy of the ordering indicator, fixing partial information decomposition (PID) for every redundancy measure satisfying the Williams-Beer axioms and invariant under injective re-encoding. Second, cross-site invariance of representation-conditional outcomes is a falsifiable implication of biological invariance taken jointly with exclusion and representation-sufficiency conditions, but not an identification strategy: we construct observationally equivalent models that disagree about biological invariance, whether the invariance test fails or passes. For binary physiology this happens exactly when case mix differs, the representation is lossy, and physiology affects the outcome. A worked example yields a 9.6-percentage-point cross-site gap under an unchanged biological mechanism. Tests performed for every patient remove the ordering gate, and measured tests can restore identification: biological invariance is identified when a test exactly summarizes outcome-relevant physiology, or, for binary physiology, when two tests with independent errors are available at sites with different case mix. Cross-site analyses should be reported as tests of a stated joint hypothesis.

Generative Active Learning for Label-Efficient Pan-Organ Ultrasound Diagnosis

Yaofei Duan, Yuhao Huang, Yuan Gao, Ruixiang Qi, Tianyu Zhang, Yanlin Geng

Medical ultrasound (US) enables real-time, non-invasive diagnosis, yet building US-based AI systems is limited by data scarcity, costly expert annotation, and limited cross-center generalizability. Privacy constraints and instance-level reporting hinder the collection of diverse abnormal cases and frame-level labels, while repeated retraining for new centers increases computational cost. We introduce EvoDiff, a generative active learning framework that integrates diffusion modelling with information-theoretic objectives, evolutionary search, and curriculum-based sampling to select informative samples across heterogeneous US datasets. EvoDiff formulates sample selection as a generate-to-retrieve process, in which diffusion-based optimization in feature space guided by information gain produces synthetic probes, and only the real unlabeled samples retrieved by these probes are annotated and used for downstream training. Across six benchmarks covering fifteen datasets and 16,696 images, we evaluate EvoDiff on convolutional network and transformer-based foundation model. EvoDiff approaches fully supervised performance with substantially fewer labels, outperforms active learning baselines, and improves foundation model generalization under cross-domain external validation on unseen cohorts. A reader study with six radiologists indicates the potential to support clinical annotation workflows, with a model-based estimated 24% to 84% reduction in annotation workload at matched downstream performance.

Application of Machine Learning and Deep Learning in diagnosis Covid19

Merchi, L.; Boucheham, C.; Layeb, A.; Derouiche, A.

Predicting the progression of COVID-19 in hospitalised patients was an important challenge during the pandemic, particularly when healthcare resources were limited. This study investigates the use of machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) models to predict three clinical outcomes: death, admission to the intensive care unit (ICU), and clinical status (improvement or worsening). The study uses two patient datasets: an Algerian cohort of 80 patients from the Internal Medicine Department of the University Hospital Centre of Constantine, collected between July and August 2020, and a French cohort of 99 patients collected between March and May 2020. Five ML classifiers were evaluated: logistic regression (LR), k-nearest neighbours (KNN), classification and regression trees (CART), Naive Bayes (NB), and support vector machines (SVM). Their performance was examined in combination with three feature-selection methods: analysis of variance (ANOVA), mutual information (MI), and recursive feature elimination (RFE). The results were also compared with multilayer perceptron (MLP) models using one to five hidden layers. Model evaluation included 30 repetitions of five-fold cross-validation on the training data, followed by evaluation on a separate hold-out test set. The results showed that selecting 20 features generally provided better cross-validated accuracy than selecting 30 features or using the full feature set. The best-performing classifier-feature-selection combination varied across the six dataset-outcome tasks. LR combined with RFE was selected for three tasks, LR combined with ANOVA for one task, and CART combined with ANOVA for two tasks. The selected ML models achieved test-set accuracies ranging from 0.47 to 0.95 and higher accuracy than the average MLP results in five of the six tasks. These findings suggest that ML methods can be useful for exploring clinical outcomes in hospitalised COVID-19 patients. However, the small sample sizes and limitations of the available data mean that the results should be interpreted with caution. Further evaluation using larger and independent patient datasets is needed before these models can be considered for clinical use.

DuSS-PET: Self-Supervised Low-Dose PET Reconstruction Using Pseudo-Dual-Domain Consistency Without Paired Data

Shuijin Lin, Yuzhu He, Yongbo Wang, Jianhua Ma, Fan Wang

Positron emission tomography (PET) is pivotal in medicine and healthcare, but requires high radiation doses for quality imaging. Although deep learning has shown promise for low-dose PET reconstruction/enhancement, existing methods primarily rely on fully supervised learning, which requires paired low- and full-dose scans that are clinically impractical to obtain. To overcome this fundamental limitation, we propose DuSS-PET, a novel self-supervised PET reconstruction framework that operates without any paired low- and full-dose data. Our method is built upon a computational Noisier2Noise (Nr2N) paradigm, featuring a Bernoulli corruption strategy that serves as an image-space approximation to emulate low-count noise patterns in image-derived pseudo-sinogram representations. The DuSS-PET framework integrates three core components: an adaptive Swin transformer for pseudo-sinogram restoration, a plug-and-play module for image enhancement, and a consistency-constrained SSL strategy that enforces agreement between the pseudo-sinogram and image representations. Extensive experiments on multi-center datasets demonstrate that DuSS-PET achieves superior reconstruction performance, outperforming state-of-the-art SSL methods and achieving comparable or even better results than fully supervised approaches across various dose levels. In particular, it exhibits exceptional generalization across different scanner vendors. This work is among the first self-supervised low-dose PET reconstruction framework that enforces consistency between reconstructed images and pseudo-sinograms, where the pseudo-sinogram generated in the image space serves as a solid computational consistency constraint for enhanced imaging without full-dose training scans. The source code will be publicly available at https://github.com/ladderlab-xjtu/DuSS-PET.

Target Recoverability under Selective Labels: A Failure Mode in ECG-Based Acute Coronary Syndrome Prediction

Alexander Schubert, Anton Håkansson, Ziad Obermeyer

Medical machine learning models often learn from diagnoses observed only in patients clinicians chose to test. The biases of such selective labels are well known, but less understood is what happens when a diagnosis includes cases the model's inputs cannot detect. Because testing decisions draw on information the model does not see, these cases can appear predictable among tested patients, producing relationships that fail, and can even reverse, among the untested. We formalize this mechanism and show in simulations that it worsens as testing becomes more selective or undetectable cases more common, while models trained only on detectable cases remain informative. We empirically study electrocardiogram (ECG)-based prediction of acute coronary syndrome (ACS), which is confirmed only by invasive testing and whose subtypes differ in how clearly they appear on the ECG. Three ECG foundation models fine-tuned on revascularization, our ACS proxy, performed well among catheterized patients (AUROC 0.740-0.770) but worse than chance for 30-day major adverse cardiovascular events among untested patients (AUROC 0.381-0.478). Models trained on ECG-visible subtypes retained discrimination (AUROC 0.680-0.709), whereas a model trained on unstable angina, which can occur without ECG changes, was inversely related to risk (AUROC 0.310). Under selective labels, effective learning therefore requires prediction targets that can be inferred from the model's inputs and evaluation in the intended deployment population, which often extends beyond the patients who were tested.
