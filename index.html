GraphBEAT: Graph-based Bucketized Event Aggregation Over Time for Early Risk Stratification of Gastrointestinal Cancers

Leo Y. Li-Han, Chris Varghese, Elizabeth B. Habermann, Mark J. Truty, William R. G. Perry, Cornelius A. Thiels

Longitudinal electronic health records (EHRs) contain complementary diagnostic and laboratory information about patient health. However, irregularity, sparsity, heterogeneity, and incompleteness in documentation complicate EHR modeling. To address the problem, we introduce GraphBEAT (Graph-based Bucketized Event Aggregation over Time), a temporal graph representation learning framework that groups clinical events into time buckets and transforms observed features into patient-specific, temporally directed graphs to enable early risk stratification for gastrointestinal cancers. Each node in an EHR graph encodes feature identity, observed value, and temporal position of clinical events. A context-aware, time-decay message-passing mechanism models transitions between successive buckets, followed by multi-head attention pooling to generate patient-level representations. GraphBEAT was trained and evaluated on multi-site longitudinal diagnosis codes and laboratory tests to predict pancreatic, liver, and intestinal cancer at 12-, 24-, and 36-month diagnostic lead times. In all experimental setups, GraphBEAT consistently outperformed baseline graph neural networks (GraphSAGE, GAT, and GCN) and sequence modeling approaches (Transformer and long short-term memory [LSTM]). GraphBEAT also required over 250x fewer floating-point operations per patient and over 20x fewer parameters than the Transformer and LSTM baselines. These results support GraphBEAT as an accurate and computationally efficient approach for cancer risk stratification from sparse, heterogeneous longitudinal health records.

MedPAO-VL: Interpretable and Protocol-Compliant Agent for Structured Radiology Report Generation

Shrish Shrinath Vaidya, Gowthamaan Palani, Minmini Selvam, Gokulraja Srinivasaraja, Ganapathy Krishnamurthi

Despite the remarkable generalization capabilities of Large Language Models, generating clinically accurate and structured radiology reports from medical images remains a significant challenge. Current end-to-end approaches function as black boxes, prone to factual hallucinations and generating ungrounded findings. This lack of reliability and failure to adhere to domain-specific structuring protocols, fundamentally limits their clinical utility. We present MedPAO-VL, a multimodal agent that shifts the paradigm from monolithic generation to explainable, iterative, protocol-compliant structured report generation. Unlike deterministic models, our plan-act-observe loop functions as a goal-oriented agent: it first perceives the Chest X-Ray through an explainable vision module designed to capture inherent diagnostic uncertainty, then iteratively refines the generated report by observing semantic discrepancies and self-correcting its generation for better alignment with visually detected clinical findings. Evaluation of our framework demonstrates that this agentic reasoning improves clinical correctness and completeness, achieving an expert-rated accuracy score of 3.5/5 and a structural score of 4.3/5. By prioritizing pathology specific explainability and iterative refinement over opaque end-to-end mapping, MedPAO-VL offers a transparent and clinically viable alternative for automated structured radiology report generation.

Tool-Augmented Large Language Models for Reliable Medical Calculation from Patient Notes

Yang Zhong, Ling Luo, Xuanlyu Zhu, Yanhua Wang, Yuanyuan Sun, Jian Wang

Medical calculators translate raw patient observations into clinically actionable values and scores. However, applying these calculators directly to free-form patient notes requires precise variable extraction combined with the exact execution of complex formulas, scoring rules, and output conventions. Relying solely on direct large language model (LLM) prompting is unreliable in this context, as rule recall, parameter extraction, arithmetic reasoning, and formatting become entangled within a single generative step. To address these limitations, we propose a tool-augmented LLM framework for reliable medical calculation from patient notes. The framework retrieves and structures calculator specifications, generates reusable executable tools, and uses a constrained extractor to map each patient note to calculator-ready inputs. The tools then perform deterministic calculation, separating clinical language understanding from rule execution and arithmetic. Evaluated on the MedCalc-Bench dataset, our method achieves 86.73% final-answer accuracy, outperforming the one-shot prompting and supervised fine tuning-only baselines by 24.0 and 17.27 percentage points. Ablation and error analyses show that these performance gains are primarily driven by rule-grounded tool synthesis and schema-constrained extraction, whereas remaining failures are concentrated in clinical-semantic extraction and benchmark-specific output contracts. By grounding calculator logic in executable tools, the proposed approach alleviates arithmetic hallucinations and improves the computational reliability of the final clinical outputs.

PAINT: A Framework for Multilingual Pain Representation from Medical Licensing Exams to Real-World Clinical Texts

Boya Zhang, Yuanchao Ma, Jiageng Wu, Krittaphas Chaisutyakorn, Rodrigo Gameiro, Ahram Han

Pain is one of the most common and clinically important symptoms, reflecting disease onset, progression, and treatment response. However, pain is difficult to accurately capture and interpret from medical text, as its expression varies across languages, documentation types, and patient demographics, and is often embedded in terminology, negation, temporality, and contextual descriptions. Existing pain-related studies remain largely monolingual or limited to narrow clinical settings. In this study, we introduce PAINT, a multilingual framework for studying pain representation across medical exams and real-world clinical texts. PAINT includes nine pain-focused dataset partitions spanning English, Spanish, Portuguese, and Japanese, with clinician annotations in all four languages. We curated a structured schema covering pain relevance, pain presence, anatomical location, pain descriptors, and demographic information. To capture multilingual pain representations, we leverage large language models (LLMs) and adopt an ensemble strategy to improve performance. Ten clinicians across four languages validate the labels: the ensemble reaches macro-F1 of 0.91-1.00 on pain relevance and pain presence and 92-100% accuracy on demographics, with inter-annotator $κ$ of 0.82-1.00. We release the WorldMedQA-Pain ensemble labels, the extraction pipeline, validation scripts, and clinician annotation sheets for publicly licensed corpora, providing a foundation for pain-centered clinical NLP and for studying pain documentation across languages and healthcare contexts.

ProCARE: Real-World Study Automation via Profile-Grounded Evidence Contracts

Zhaoyu Fan, Bowen Han, Jincheng Ou, Yanwei Ren, Zehua He, Kaiyu Huang

Real-world studies (RWS) analyze routinely collected healthcare data, such as electronic health records and insurance claims, to estimate treatment effects, monitor safety, and identify risk factors outside randomized trials. Automating RWS with large language model agents is appealing but brittle: valid analyses depend on dataset-specific clinical semantics, including index dates, follow-up windows, patient/event-level units, and outcome boundaries that are often implicit in observational data. This leads to schema mismatches, temporal errors, inconsistent analytical granularity, and unsupported clinical claims. We present ProCARE, a profile-grounded framework that reformulates RWS automation as constrained compilation. ProCARE converts heterogeneous data profiles into Evidence Contracts specifying available variables, temporal attributes, distributions, and evidentiary limits, and compiles study objectives into RWS-IR, a strongly typed intermediate representation for variables, time relations, analysis units, and statistical tasks. Deterministic validation and localized repair then check and revise generated workflows across schema mapping, cohort construction, variable derivation, analysis, and interpretation. On 36 EHR-based benchmark tasks, ProCARE outperforms strong LLM-agent baselines in code executability, semantic consistency, and conclusion traceability, with automated violation diagnoses showing moderate-to-substantial agreement with blinded expert review.

SCCL-DT: Semantic-Conflict-Aware Contrastive Learning with Dual-Rule Teacher Distillation for Zero-Shot Chest X-Ray Diagnosis

Ting Liu, Haoran Lai, HaobinChen, Ming Liu, S Kevin Zhou

Contrastive vision-language pre-training supports zero-shot chest X-ray diagnosis, but standard objectives treat every off-diagonal image-report pair in a batch as a negative. In radiology, recurring findings across patients mean that instance mismatch need not imply semantic incompatibility, creating false-negative supervision that we term a semantic conflict. A training-trace audit shows that pair-observations selected by the online mask used during training are enriched for report-derived finding agreement, although this proxy analysis does not establish clinical equivalence. Fixed thresholds can become miscalibrated as similarity distributions evolve, while soft reweighting leaves residual repulsive gradients. We therefore propose Semantic-Conflict-Aware Contrastive Learning (SCCL), which tracks modality-specific similarity distributions with exponential-moving-average (EMA) statistics, derives adaptive thresholds, and hard-masks flagged candidates before softmax normalization. Inclusive (OR) and consensus (AND) rules define two SCCL teachers, whose bidirectional cross-modal rankings are consolidated through standard distillation into a single deployable student, SCCL-DT. Across five public chest X-ray benchmarks, SCCL-DT achieves a mean zero-shot AUC of $0.850$, compared with $0.837$ for the strongest evaluated baseline in our comparison. Code will be made publicly available soon.

SMI: Ontology-Grounded Semantic IDs for Hierarchy-Aware Medical Concept Representation

Ziyang Song, Lia Shen, Yixuan Li, Qincheng Lu, Weihao Li, Haoran Xu

Biomedical language models (LMs) provide strong representations for clinical tasks, but they often fail to capture the hierarchical structure among medical concepts. This limitation is especially pronounced for rare and fine-grained clinical codes in long-tailed vocabularies, where hierarchy-agnostic LM representations become less informative as concept frequency decreases. We introduce Semantic Medical Identifier (SMI), a training-free framework for constructing ontology-grounded Semantic IDs and hierarchy-aware medical concept representations. For each diagnosis, procedure, or medication concept, SMI defines its Semantic ID as the coarse-to-fine path through an expert-defined medical ontology. SMI then aggregates frozen biomedical LM embeddings across the corresponding ontology levels to form hierarchy-aware medical concept representations that preserve coarse-to-fine clinical semantics. The base SMI uses uniform aggregation across ontology levels, while a level-weighted variant selects global level weights on validation data. Our theoretical analysis shows that SMI yields more informative medical concept representations than hierarchy-agnostic LM embeddings, particularly for rare and fine-grained clinical concepts. Experiments show that SMI captures meaningful hierarchical structure and improves clinical relation prediction. On MIMIC-IV, SMI improves mortality prediction, readmission prediction, drug recommendation, and rare drug prediction. On eICU, SMI yields more robust patient representations under cross-hospital distribution shift.

Sequence Composition in EHR Foundation Model Pretraining

Autoregressive electronic health record (EHR) foundation models predict the next event token in patient trajectories: sequences of structured medical events. A typical pretraining strategy is to concatenate all trajectories into a global stream and sample token windows from it. This gives patients with longer sequences more training exposure and allows tokens to attend across patient boundaries. We argue that these choices create a misalignment between pretraining and downstream clinical tasks. To explore this, we investigate how the composition of pretraining sequences affects clinical prediction performance. Using the global-stream approach as a baseline, we propose alternative sequence composition methods that control how patient trajectories contribute to pretraining sequences. Across four MIMIC-IV v2.2 tasks, our best method improves macro AUROC, AUPRC, F1, and balanced accuracy, with relative gains of 9.3% in F1 and 2.7% in AUPRC under a fixed training recipe. We find that these gains generalize across four additional evaluation settings, including the INSPIRE and eICU-CRD datasets, with improvements in macro AUROC, AUPRC, and F1 score. Our results show that simply changing how EHR data are presented to the model during pretraining can improve downstream performance, without changing the model or collecting additional data.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first benchmark to compare multiple foundation models under a common adult-to-pediatric evaluation setting while also analyzing the effect of adaptation depth. We harmonize VinDr-CXR and PediCXR into a five-label classification task and evaluate six foundation models and two CNN baselines. Another important finding is that increasing the depth of adaptation does not significantly improve cross-population generalization. Moreover, strong in-domain performance does not translate into better performance on pediatric data.

Reinforcement Learning In Partially Verifiable Environments for Colon Cancer Treatment Planning

Li-Ching Chen, Andrés Franco Valiente, Yuta Ishiyama, Zachary R. Chalmers, William Chen, Kelsey Natsuhara

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong medical question-answering capabilities but extending these advances to clinical reasoning remains challenging because many clinical tasks are only partially verifiable. Recent clinical reinforcement learning (RL) work has therefore largely focused on diagnostic endpoints, where correctness can be more directly verified. Oncology treatment decision making is a particularly challenging instance of this problem that extends beyond medical question-answering in which multiple decision trajectories can be clinically defensible for the same partially observed patient state. We formulate oncology treatment planning as an interactive RL problem under partial observability and construct patient-specific, process-aware rewards from decision processes observed in real world clinical records.
